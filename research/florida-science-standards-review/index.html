A full-draft comparison of Florida’s proposed 2026 B.E.S.T. Science standards with adopted Florida standards and selected external references, with source lineage, omissions, grade placement, process records, and metadata reviewed separately.

This site is an independent analysis and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Florida Department of Education. The working findings distinguish direct wording matches, conceptual continuity, partial coverage, possible omissions, and unresolved cases. A missing source match is not evidence that a benchmark is original. Review of pacing within the school year is not possible from the public packet: its “timeline” is historical, not an instructional calendar.

Headline finding: evolution and climate change are politically salient omissions

Florida’s 2008 standards adopted evolution after a contested, last-minute wording change; they also explicitly required students to connect Earth-system and astronomical factors to global climate change. Later reporting documented climate-term restrictions in state agencies under Rick Scott and state-requested climate-content edits to textbooks under Ron DeSantis. The 2026 draft now omits “evolution” from all numbered benchmarks and mentions “climate change” only once in supporting text, not a benchmark. Related science remains, but the history makes these changes a central public question. It does not prove the 2026 workgroup’s motive.

Evolution framing: The draft teaches natural selection, fossils, and evolutionary relationships, but does not name common descent as their unifying scientific explanation. The review treats that omission as sectarianly consequential and aligned with antievolution opposition to shared ancestry, while distinguishing that curricular effect from evidence of author intent.

Other specific changes: The 2026 draft has no benchmark equivalent to the 2008 high-school expectations on human evolution. It teaches radiometric dating of rocks but does not explicitly ask students to determine Earth’s age; it retains evidence for an expanding universe without naming the Big Bang or the universe’s age. The new packet is also much longer, though the review does not infer instructional overload without pacing and classroom-time evidence.

How to read the findings

Corpus-bounded, not a finding of misconduct

“Non-divergent” means no informative divergence was identified against the research corpus used; it does not mean no outside source exists. Similarity can reflect common scientific content or a shared upstream source. The public PDFs are flattened exports without tracked changes or individual source attribution; their metadata does not resolve who drafted or supplied text. The reports distinguish those limits from evidence and flag where further records would be needed.

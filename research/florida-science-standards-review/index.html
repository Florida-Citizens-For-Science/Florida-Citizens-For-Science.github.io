A visual guide to the review

What changed in Florida’s proposed science standards?

Florida is reviewing new K–12 science standards. This independent comparison looks at what the 2026 draft teaches, what it leaves out, and how it relates to earlier standards and public records.

These are proposals, not adopted standards. This review is independent of the Florida Department of Education. It describes the documents examined and does not establish who wrote individual benchmarks or why. Its omission findings focus on coded standard descriptions and numbered benchmarks; supporting text is analyzed separately. How the source layers and adoption status are treated (new tab).

01 / The headline findings

Evolution and climate change are largely unnamed

Both subjects have related science in the draft. The concern is that major explanations are not stated in the coded standards and numbered benchmark statements. Clarifications and examples are analyzed separately because their status in a final rule must be checked.

variation→selection→change over time

Evolution

Mechanisms appear; the unifying explanation does not

No numbered benchmark uses “evolution” biologically; one parent standard uses “landscape evolution” geologically. The coded text does not name common ancestry as the explanation connecting evidence, or retain the 2008 human-evolution expectations.

Climate change

One clarification mention; none in coded standards

“Climate change” appears once in a benchmark clarification, not in a coded standard description or numbered benchmark. Climate science remains, but some explicit attribution and forecasting tasks are absent.

See the placement crosswalk (opens in a new tab)

Why these stand out: Florida’s 2008 adoption included a contested last-minute evolution wording change and explicit climate-change expectations. Later reporting described state climate-language restrictions and textbook edits. That history raises a legitimate question about the new omissions; it does not prove the 2026 workgroup’s motive. 02 / The scope

A full-draft review, with a few open cases

The comparison inventoried every leaf benchmark in the three 2026 grade-band PDFs and checked it against the defined research corpus. This count covers the PDF standards packet, not yet every item offered for public review.

The mirror captured visible text from all 26 online forms and found 11 numbered K–12 Scientific Thinking and Reasoning Standards (STARS) absent from the three grade-band PDFs and the current register. Those standards form a separate set; the 1,512 figure below counts only PDF leaf benchmarks and is not a total of every item in the public-review packet. One K–5 form remains only partially captured. 04 / The public record

The history makes the wording consequential

2008

Florida adopts science standards after a public dispute over evolution wording; climate-change expectations are included.

Rick Scott administration

News reporting describes state agency limits on climate-related language and scientists’ public speech.

Ron DeSantis administration

Reporting describes state-requested climate-content changes in school textbooks.

2026 proposal

No coded statement names biological evolution; the parent phrase “landscape evolution” is geological. Climate change appears once in a clarification, not coded standard text.

This chronology establishes that the subjects have a political history in Florida. It cannot, by itself, establish intent behind the 2026 draft. The source page separates documented events from interpretation and identifies remaining limits.

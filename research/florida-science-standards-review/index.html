A visual guide to the review

What changed in Florida’s proposed science standards?

Florida is reviewing new K–12 science standards. This independent comparison looks at what the 2026 draft teaches, what it leaves out, and how it relates to earlier standards and public records.

These are proposals, not adopted standards. This review is independent of the Florida Department of Education. It describes the documents examined and does not establish who wrote individual benchmarks or why.

Start with the headline findings ↓

01 / The headline findings

Evolution and climate change are largely unnamed

Both subjects have related science in the draft. The change is that major ideas students would be expected to explain are not stated directly in numbered benchmarks.

variation→selection→change over time

Evolution

Mechanisms appear; the unifying explanation does not

The draft includes natural selection, fossils, and evolutionary relationships. It does not name common descent as the explanation connecting them, and it has no numbered benchmark equivalent to the 2008 human-evolution expectations.

Climate change

One supporting-text mention; no benchmark

Earth and space science remain in the draft, but “climate change” appears once in supporting text, not as a numbered benchmark. Explicit attribution and forecasting tasks are also absent.

See the placement crosswalk (opens in a new tab)

Why these stand out: Florida’s 2008 adoption included a contested last-minute evolution wording change and explicit climate-change expectations. Later reporting described state climate-language restrictions and textbook edits. That history raises a legitimate question about the new omissions; it does not prove the 2026 workgroup’s motive. 04 / The public record

The history makes the wording consequential

2008

Florida adopts science standards after a public dispute over evolution wording; climate-change expectations are included.

Rick Scott administration

News reporting describes state agency limits on climate-related language and scientists’ public speech.

Ron DeSantis administration

Reporting describes state-requested climate-content changes in school textbooks.

2026 proposal

Evolution is absent from numbered benchmarks; climate change appears once in supporting text.

This chronology establishes that the subjects have a political history in Florida. It cannot, by itself, establish intent behind the 2026 draft. The source page separates documented events from interpretation and identifies remaining limits.

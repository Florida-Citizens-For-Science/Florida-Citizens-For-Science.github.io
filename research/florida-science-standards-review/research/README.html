Scope and source integrity

The reviewed public draft is the Florida Department of Education (FDOE) packet labeled for review. FDOE describes these documents as proposed drafts, not adopted standards. The packet comprises the three grade-band PDFs, the foreword, and supplemental K–12 tables. The copies in this folder were downloaded from FDOE’s public CDN on October 21, 2026 and identified by SHA-256 below. The three main grade-band PDFs carry PDF creation/modification timestamps from September 30; the foreword and tables from September 25. Those are export/document timestamps, not a full history of edits or when text was authored.

The user’s ChatGPT analysis, /home/netuser/Downloads/20261021-florida-science-standards-evolution.md, is a research plan for a source-by-source variorum, not a completed provenance investigation. It correctly stresses comparing benchmark text, clarifications, examples, foreword and tables, and distinguishes the 2007 Florida Citizens for Science proposal from adopted FDOE standards. This review confirmed the FDOE source packet and added an initial metadata/content check. The plan’s broad comparative claims and proposed source lineage have not all been independently tested.

One useful update from checking the draft directly: climate change occurs in SC.912.A.12.8, in a list of human impacts on mammals. Any shorthand that the phrase is entirely absent would need correction, though the single occurrence does not establish the extent of climate-science instruction.

The Grammarly identifiers are a concrete third-party processing marker in two of five PDFs. A technical analysis by Didier Stevens reports that Grammarly inserts this metadata when its Word add-in checks a document, while warning the marker could persist from an older source document. Thus it supports that the source documents passed through Grammarly at some point, but does not establish that the current draft text was submitted, what Grammarly did, who invoked it, or whether this was authorized. FDOE’s Word source files and Grammarly usage/contract or account records are needed to resolve that.

These are flattened/exported PDFs; their lack of tracked changes says nothing conclusive about tracked changes in the original DOCX files. Initial content and lineage observations

  • The draft does include explicit evolution-related content. Grade 8 benchmarks address natural selection, inherited variation, differential survival/reproduction, and evidence from fossils, anatomy, and genetics. Grade 9–12 material includes natural selection and adaptation, and refers to the evolution of photosynthetic strategies. Therefore, a claim that the draft entirely removes evolution or natural selection would be inaccurate. How prominently these ideas are taught compared with the adopted 2008 standards remains a separate coverage question.
  • The exact phrase “climate change” occurs in the 9–12 PDF at SC.912.A.12.8, in a list of anthropogenic impacts on mammals. This is a mention, not evidence that climate change receives broad or central coverage.
  • The 2007 Florida Citizens for Science publication labels itself “A Suggested Basis for Discussion and Revision” and a project of Florida Citizens for Science, Inc.; it is an alternative proposal, not an adopted state standard. Its high-school biology section contains a substantial “Evolution” strand, including natural selection, genetic variation, fossils, and comparative anatomy. Similar concepts in the 2026 draft can be traced to a public Florida science-standards discussion by 2007, but no distinctive copied wording or direct transmission from this proposal has yet been demonstrated.
  • The 2008 adopted Florida standards also explicitly cover evolution and natural selection. Their biology specifications describe inherited variation, overproduction, differential reproductive success, and natural selection. The current draft’s treatment has recognizable continuity with these established Florida standards.
  • Source comparison found an exact seven-word sequence in 2026 SC.7.SL.2.1—“births, deaths, immigration, emigration, and limiting factors”—that also appears in the 2008 Florida population benchmark SC.912.L.17.5. This supports likely predecessor wording lineage with a grade shift, while leaving the specific drafting route unknown. Separate near-verbatim matches tie two other draft passages to the NRC/NGSS lineage. The ranked comparison and limitations are in alternative-source-analysis.md.
  • The current draft’s grade 8 survival/reproduction language has conceptual overlap with NGSS MS-LS4-4. The wording is not verbatim. Such overlap is consistent with common science-education content and does not establish copying or outside influence.
  • Searches of the public draft for common advocacy phrases (“intelligent design,” “creationism,” “teach the controversy,” “academic freedom,” “common ancestry,” “common descent”) found no hits. This is a bounded keyword check, not an exhaustive search for indirect influence.
  • A separate FDOE press release launching the 2026 review invokes the classical tradition, “the book of creation,” and cultivation of virtue. This is relevant context for the administration’s public framing, not evidence that the standards text contains a sectarian insertion or that the workgroup adopted the phrasing; the source and limits are documented in the process comparison and rhetoric audit.
  • FDOE’s 2024 call for workgroup applicants expressly contemplated Florida-certified teachers, district science specialists, and postsecondary science instructors. Outside participation by selected educators or scientists would therefore be expected; the unresolved questions are who participated, in what capacity, and what drafts or comments they contributed.

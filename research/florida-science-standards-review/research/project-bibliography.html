Instructional-burden commentary

  1. Sampson, Victor. “What Do I Think of Florida’s Proposed Science Standards? Let’s Do the Math.” Argument-Driven Inquiry, October 6, 2026. Read the article.

    Source note

    Scenario analysis of instructional time implied by the high-school draft. Its strongest, verified point is that the draft foreword describes benchmarks, clarifications, and examples together as expected outcomes carrying the full weight of the standards, and expects mastery by the end of a grade/course. The 9–12 text extraction independently yields 3,935 numbered clarification labels; 3,919 contain “Instruction includes.” Those counts describe document structure, not separate lesson topics. The 24/73-credit estimates count every clarification as one topic, assume fixed periods per topic, and place every grade-9–12 expectation within the three graduation-required science credits. The foreword calls for integrating related benchmarks; it also includes modeling, mathematics, laboratories, and other scientific practices in its instructional framework, so practice-linked clarifications cannot be treated as having zero instructional cost. The statute does not assign the full grade-band set to those three credits. We cite the article as a useful challenge and scenario model, not as evidence that Florida has a proven statewide workload shortfall. The article is linked and summarized here; its copyrighted text is not reproduced.

The NAS model standards are now included as a complete, private source in the research corpus. The comparison found broad topic overlap and important differences in explicit treatment, but no distinctive Florida–Franklin wording match or documentary evidence of drafting influence. Larry Plank's article is included as an attributed interpretation that prompted this test, not as proof of copying or adoption. The source PDF is copyrighted and is not redistributed here.

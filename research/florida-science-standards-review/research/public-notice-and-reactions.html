Public notice and reactions to the 2026 draft

The draft has been publicly available for review since September 25, 2026. Notices, session recordings, local reporting, and organizational commentary document a debate over explicit science expectations, instructional load, scientific inquiry, and public participation.

What the notice establishes: FDOE’s public portal labels the review period “September – October 2026,” and currently lists four listening opportunities, the last held October 27. The portal does not state a day-of-month deadline for written comments. Reporting published October 29 says the public still had an opportunity to shape the draft before expected State Board consideration in December. Check the current FDOE portal for any update.

How notice and coverage unfolded

  1. September 25 — FDOE launches the review. The Department announced a “comprehensive rewrite,” described its goals as coherent, content-rich, evidence-based science standards, and announced three listening sessions. The current portal later added an October 27 Orange County opportunity. Official notices describe review-only drafts, not approved standards.
  2. September 28–October 1 — the first reporting and meetings. WUSF previewed the input opportunities and reported concern among education advocates that climate-change instruction could be reduced. WLRN’s October 1 coverage of the meetings reported disagreement: some participants said underlying concepts remained despite terminology changes; others objected to the absence of familiar terms and asked for explicit evolution and climate expectations. It also included an educator who welcomed emphasis on modeling, data, laboratory practice, and evidence.
  3. October 9–15 — comment guidance and a public defense. Educator Bertha Vazquez published a step-by-step guide encouraging comments about evolution and climate language. At an October 14 State Board meeting, Commissioner Henry Mack defended the draft’s emphasis on evidence and non-ideological science and said evolution and climate science remained in the standards. WLRN also reported the Florida Education Association’s call for research-led standards, broader participation, and more time for review.
  4. October 27–29 — final listening session and updated responses. The Florida Department of State published an October 14 meeting notice for the October 27 Orange County session. An October 28 NCSE account said reporting had not identified the revisions FDOE said it had made after receiving nearly 1,800 comments. Central Florida Public Media’s October 29 report summarized criticism about explicit evolution and human-caused climate-change expectations, nature-of-science language, and workload. It also reported support from attendees who favored questioning models and respectful disagreement.

Reading the record carefully

  • FDOE’s portal identifies the draft as proposed and not approved; the official October 27 meeting notice identifies the subject as “Science State Academic Standards.”
  • As of this October 29 check, the official portal gives a month-range rather than an exact written-comment closing date. News coverage describes comments as still open during the October 27 session. We therefore do not supply an unsupported October 31 deadline.
  • The reported “nearly 1,800” figure is the Department’s comment count as relayed at the listening session. The sources reviewed here do not provide the submissions, a breakdown by subject, or a public response matrix showing which revisions followed which comments.
  • News stories select representative speakers; organization pages express the organization’s position. Neither supplies a representative poll or a full transcript of every comment. The Florida Channel’s September 30 recording is a primary audiovisual record of the virtual public hearing.
  • The reactions inform public context. They do not replace the benchmark-by-benchmark comparison or establish the motives behind the drafting decisions.

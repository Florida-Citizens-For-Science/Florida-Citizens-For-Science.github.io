Plain-language overview

Florida’s proposed science standards keep instruction about natural selection, fossils, climate systems, and human effects on Earth. The draft also changes what it names and requires: it does not use the word “evolution” in any numbered standard, omits the earlier standards’ specific human-evolution expectations, and changes how some climate and Earth-history topics are framed. The packet is much longer than the 2008 standards, but page count alone cannot show whether teachers have enough time to teach it. Public PDFs do not include the draft history and comment records that would explain how the text developed. The evidence and qualifications are in the plain-language brief and full report.

Overview

Florida’s proposed science standards keep natural selection, fossils, climate systems, and human impacts on Earth. But they also change what is named and required, and the public record leaves major questions about how the draft was written. Here’s a short overview: Read the brief.

Evolution

Florida’s draft still teaches natural selection and fossils. But none of its numbered standards uses the word “evolution,” and it drops the 2008 standards’ specific lessons on human evolution. The public documents don’t explain why. Read more.

Climate

Climate science has not disappeared from Florida’s draft. But “climate change” appears only once, in a supporting note—not in a numbered standard—and “global warming” does not appear. That’s a change in wording, not proof that all climate instruction is gone. Read more.

Volume

Florida’s three proposed grade-band science documents total 987 pages, compared with 74 pages for the 2008 standards. Page counts don’t prove the new draft is too much to teach. They do raise a practical question: how will teachers fit it all into the school year? Read more.

Process

The public draft PDFs don’t include tracked changes, successive drafts, meeting minutes, or notes showing where standards came from. Those records would help the public understand how the standards changed and how feedback was handled. Read more.

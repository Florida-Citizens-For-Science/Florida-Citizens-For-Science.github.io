What is named, and where

  • Evolution: A high-school benchmark names evolutionary processes in marine organisms; its clarification includes common ancestry. The coded requirement is limited to marine-life diversity, adaptation, and distribution. A targeted search did not locate equivalents of the 2008 human-ancestry and fossil-hominid outcomes; the full predecessor crosswalk is pending.
  • Climate change: The phrase appears once in a benchmark clarification, not in a coded standard or numbered benchmark. The phrase appeared in four 2008 benchmark statements. The detailed task comparison against the October 26 draft is pending; first-round claims about climate mechanisms, attribution, and forecasting have not yet been rechecked against this version.
  • Earth and universe ages: Supporting guidance gives Earth’s age as approximately 4 billion years and the universe’s age as approximately 13.8 billion years. These are present in the second-round proposal, though not in the coded benchmark sentences. “Big Bang” is not named in the three grade-band PDFs.

These are differences in scope and placement across layers—not a claim that all related science has disappeared. The FDOE foreword says clarifications and examples help define expected outcomes and “carry the full weight of the standards”; final legal status depends on the adopted rule and files. Plain-language overview

Florida’s October 26 second-round draft names evolutionary processes in a marine-life benchmark, and its clarification includes common ancestry. A targeted search did not locate equivalents of two 2008 human-evolution outcomes; the full predecessor crosswalk is pending. “Climate change” appears once in a clarification, not a coded benchmark; a full task-level climate comparison against this version is also pending. The FDOE foreword says supporting clarifications and examples carry the full weight of expected outcomes, so their placement matters too. Florida should explain how these pieces fit together.

Florida’s history makes these choices politically salient. Evolution wording was contested during adoption of the 2008 standards; later reports described climate-term restrictions under Rick Scott and climate-content edits requested for textbooks under Ron DeSantis. That history warrants scrutiny but does not prove why the 2026 workgroup made these choices. The historical evidence note and project bibliography document the sources and limits.

Evolution

Florida’s 2008 standards called evolution biology’s organizing principle. The Oct. 26 draft names evolutionary processes in one marine-life benchmark, and its clarification includes common ancestry. A targeted search did not locate equivalents of two high-school outcomes on human ancestry and fossil hominids; the full crosswalk is pending. The coded requirement does not state a broader cross-life organizing account. Read the comparison and source notes.

Human evolution

The 2008 Florida standards explicitly asked high-school students to examine human ancestry and what fossil hominids show about human evolution. A targeted search did not locate equivalent 2026 wording; the full current-version crosswalk is pending. See the evidence and limits.

Climate change

Florida’s 2008 standards used “climate change” in four benchmark statements. In the Oct. 26 draft, the phrase appears once in a clarification, not in coded standard or benchmark text. A full comparison of climate tasks against this version is still underway; earlier claims about attribution and forecasting are not yet verified for the second round. Read the detailed crosswalk.

Why the history matters

Florida adopted evolution in 2008 after a contested last-minute wording change. Later reporting described climate-term restrictions under Rick Scott and state-requested climate edits to textbooks under Ron DeSantis. That record makes today’s choices about human-evolution expectations and climate wording politically consequential; it does not prove the 2026 workgroup’s motive. Read the sourced chronology.

Franklin Standards comparison

We compared Florida’s Oct. 26 draft with the 2024 Franklin Standards directly. Florida’s guidance states Earth’s and the universe’s approximate ages; a marine benchmark names evolutionary processes, and its clarification includes common ancestry. “Big Bang” is not named. We found no distinctive extended wording match or adoption record. Read the comparison and limits.

College preparation

FDOE says this rewrite should prepare students for college-level science. Do the draft’s required outcomes line up? A lack of a located university statement after five weeks tells us little; silence is not assent. Ask FDOE and Florida universities for a standards-to-entry-course crosswalk. UC’s 2008 case is a narrow course-approval precedent, not Florida credit rejection. Read the evidence and limits.

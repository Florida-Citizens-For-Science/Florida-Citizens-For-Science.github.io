What is named, and where

  • Evolution: The November 20 draft explicitly names evolution in two benchmarks, one middle-school and one high-school. A high-school benchmark adds fossil-hominid examples and a clarification naming common ancestry and descent with modification. These changes revise the earlier October 26 wording. The 2008 standards’ broader organizing statement remains a useful scope comparison.
  • Climate change: The phrase appears three times in the November 20 DOCX: once in a benchmark clarification and twice in supporting tables; no numbered benchmark statement uses it. “Global warming” is absent. The draft also contains anthropogenic Earth-system expectations. The detailed task comparison on attribution and forecasting is tied to the October 26 review set.
  • Earth and universe ages: Supporting guidance gives Earth’s age as approximately 4 billion years and the universe’s age as approximately 13.8 billion years. These are present in the second-round proposal, though not in the coded benchmark sentences. “Big Bang” is not named in the three grade-band PDFs.

These are differences in scope and placement across layers—not a claim that all related science has disappeared. The FDOE foreword says clarifications and examples help define expected outcomes and “carry the full weight of the standards”; final legal status depends on the adopted rule and files. Why the history matters

Florida adopted evolution in 2008 after a contested last-minute wording change. Later reporting described climate-term restrictions under Rick Scott and state-requested climate edits to textbooks under Ron DeSantis. That record makes today’s choices about human-evolution expectations and climate wording politically consequential; it does not prove the 2026 workgroup’s motive. Read the sourced chronology.

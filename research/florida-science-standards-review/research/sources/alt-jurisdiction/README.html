Alternative-jurisdiction source comparison

Research scope: check the most plausible non-Florida standards sources against the current draft wording. The comparison treats shared scientific content as a concept match, not proof of copying. An exact phrase or a distinctive phrase sequence is required for a verified lift.

Strongest independent candidate: Washington 2009

The official document is Washington State K–12 Science Learning Standards, Version 1.2, OSPI Document 09-0015, formally adopted in 2009. The legacy official URL cited for it is http://www.k12.wa.us/Science/pubdocs/WAScienceStandards.PDF. An indexed full-PDF copy was available at educatoral.com/documents/WAScienceStandards.pdf, and the text was inspected at PDF pages 67–68 (document pp. 60–61). The OSPI-authored PDF states that it can be downloaded from the agency website. OSPI’s later official science page confirms its 2013 successor is NGSS, distinguishing the 2009 framework from that later adoption: OSPI science standards page.

Washington, grade 6–8, standard SYSB: “The boundaries of a system can be drawn differently depending on the features of the system being investigated, the size of the system, and the purpose of the investigation.” SYSD: “In an open system, matter flows into and out of the system. In a closed system, energy may flow into or out of the system, but matter stays within the system.” SYSE continues with equal versus unequal inputs and outputs changing the amount in a system. The standard asks students to classify an open/closed system and measure flows to predict system change.

Florida, Grade 6, PDF page 1, SC.6.EN.1.1–1.3:

SC.6.EN.1.1 “Identify the characteristics of an open system.” Clarification 1 defines an open system as “one in which both energy and matter can be exchanged.” Clarification 2 calls for identifying/representing system boundaries and energy/matter transfers with models showing inputs and outputs.

SC.6.EN.1.2 “Identify the characteristics of a closed system.” Clarification 1 defines it as one in which “energy can be transferred but matter is not exchanged”; Clarification 2 again specifies system boundaries, energy transfer, models, and inputs/outputs.

SC.6.EN.1.3 “Distinguish between an open and closed system.”

Similarity assessment: this is an unusually close alignment in grade band, topic, order, and conceptual partition: boundary definition; open/closed systems; whether energy and matter cross a boundary; and inputs/outputs. However, the wording is not a textual match beyond ordinary terms such as “open system,” “closed system,” “energy,” “matter,” “boundary,” “inputs,” and “outputs.” The Florida definitions recast flows as exchange/transfer and material staying inside as material not exchanged. Those are conventional thermodynamic definitions and cannot, by themselves, establish borrowing. The WA standard’s specific system-boundary progression makes it a worthwhile comparator, but this pass classifies it as parallel content, not a verified lift.

Lineage caveat: Washington’s 2009 standards predate NGSS and therefore are not derived from the 2013 NGSS. That makes WA a separate state-document candidate. But the WA framework itself cites national science-education work (including NRC/AAAS materials); it is not wholly independent of the broader NRC lineage. Shared content could reflect those common intellectual sources or standard science education conventions. It does not establish Florida access or use.

Provenance check in Florida materials

  • The preserved 2007 Florida portal resource list, as summarized in ../../corpus-and-process.md and linked from ../../README.md, names Indiana, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Singapore, TIMSS, PISA, NAEP, NRC/NSES, and AAAS/Project 2061. It does not name Washington.
  • The 2008 standards foreword names Massachusetts, Singapore, South Carolina, and Indiana K–8, as well as national sources. It does not name Washington.
  • Search of the 2026 draft extracts and the locally preserved 2007/2008 source materials found no Washington/OSPI source citation. Incidental phrase “George Washington” in a 2026 historical timeline is unrelated.
  • Therefore, WA is a content comparator discovered through matching, not a documented source accessed by the Florida group. No access, authorship, or misconduct inference is warranted.

Search method and self-audit

The local Massachusetts 2006, NGSS DCI-arrangement, and South Carolina 2014 texts were compared against each extracted 2026 draft using normalized exact word shingles (8- and 10-word runs), then the highest-scoring overlaps were checked in context. The candidates were rejected where they were generic science language (e.g., energy forms, systems, models, or evidence) or isolated descriptive strings. This scan is a recall-limited aid: it can miss paraphrases and different clause ordering. The Washington comparison was manually checked against the exact grade-6 draft passage and source standard IDs above. Neither concept overlap nor order/sequence similarity alone was counted as a lift.

Washington source retrieval is not stored as a local PDF in this folder because sandbox network name resolution failed during attempted download. Its source is therefore linked above and directly inspectable in the browser record; a local SHA-256 is unavailable. Local source-copy SHA-256 values are recorded in the parent SHA256SUMS.txt for the PDFs that were successfully obtained.

Newly recovered historical source controls

Indiana (K–8, adopted 2000–01)

The nine ERIC copies of Indiana’s grade-level academic standards booklets for Kindergarten through Grade 8 have been added as PDFs and extracted text under this folder (indiana-2000-k.pdf and indiana-2000-grade-1..8.pdf with corresponding .txt files). ERIC describes these as Indiana Department of Education publications adopted by the Indiana State Board of Education in 2000–01; the booklets were published in August 2002. This is a version-compatible comparator for the “Indiana (K–8)” source named in the Florida 2008 foreword. The source pages identify Indiana standards, not a separate named science-only authoring group. The grades K–8 science sections are now available for corpus-wide comparison. The 2002 date is the booklet publication/retrieval date, not evidence that this exact format was available to Florida writers in 2007; the underlying standards were adopted in 2000.

An eight-token normalized exact-shingle scan against all five 2026 extracts found one shared run: “that all living things are composed of cells,” from Indiana grade-6 standard 6.4.5 and Florida draft SC.5.CB.1.1. This is a short, basic cell-theory proposition; it does not establish a distinctive wording lift. It does show a grade-band difference in the comparison documents: Indiana states the proposition at grade 6, while Florida places it in a grade-5 benchmark. A reproducible semantic-neighbor screen now covers all 412 extracted Indiana science statements; its ranks are discovery leads, not judgments. Human omission review of the Indiana set remains incomplete. See the external-source omission section for limited source-first findings and the coverage caveat.

Singapore (2008 syllabus editions, copyright 2007)

The 2008 Primary Science Syllabus (ISBN 978-981-05-8232-6) and Lower Secondary Express/Normal (Academic) Science Syllabus (ISBN 978-981-05-7760-5) are available as primary-source PDFs through the NIE Library repository and lower-secondary repository record. Their title pages give copyright year 2007 and state implementation from 2008, which is consistent with the historical input list. The publications prohibit reproduction without Ministry of Education permission, so they are recorded as web-accessible sources rather than copied into the local corpus. Their content can be checked against the primary pages; absence from the local semantic index must not be treated as a negative comparison result.

National and AAAS controls

  • The National Research Council’s National Science Education Standards (1996) is available as an official National Academies Press book with a chapter-by-chapter Read Online. The official full text is a copyrighted book; this folder records the official reader rather than redistributing a mirror PDF.
  • AAAS Project 2061’s Benchmarks Online provides both the 1993 benchmark statements and a later current version. Project 2061 states that some statements were revised during development of Atlas of Science Literacy, Volume 2; therefore the 1993 and revised versions must be distinguished. The 2007 FDOE presentation sources/2007-process/AAAS.ppt is itself retained locally, but the Atlas books are not.
  • These are known historical inputs, not yet complete offline source coverage. Use the official readers directly for comparison and identify their passage/version in any findings. Do not make a negative claim from their absence in the local semantic index.

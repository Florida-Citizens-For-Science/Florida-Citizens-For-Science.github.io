Crosswalk source-locator audit

Audit date: October 22, 2026

The benchmark register’s single-line predecessor citations were normalized to physical LF-delimited line numbers. Some earlier locators used Python splitlines() numbering, which also treats PDF form-feed page breaks as line boundaries; this created offsets between the cited number and ordinary line-numbered tools such as rg and nl.

The repair examined each citation’s standard ID, stated line number, and local source path. Where the ID appeared on the cited logical splitlines() line, the locator was converted to its physical LF line number. Citations already pointing to the correct LF-delimited line were retained. All 1,524 single-line ID/path locators now point to an existing local line containing the cited standard ID; the audit found zero missing paths, out-of-range lines, or ID mismatches.

The 125 newly added crosswalk anchors recorded as line ranges rather than single-line locators were separately checked against their exact IDs in the cited source text. The register and new deep-pass notes retain those ranges. This audit verifies locator integrity, not the semantic fit of every predecessor match; the latter remains a documented manual judgment. Source URLs, file hashes, and the full inventory are available in the corpus appendix and SHA256SUMS.txt.

Subsequent strand-pass audits

The initial totals above describe the global normalization audit before the later strand-by-strand deep reviews; they are not a cumulative count of every locator later added. Each later pass recorded its own citation check in its linked review note. In particular, the K–5 residual review checked 21 new candidate anchors, and the grades 6–8 residual review checked 8; all cited physical-LF lines were verified. The HS animal, plant, life-science, and other-strand passes and the initial K–5/middle passes likewise report per-pass line/code audits in their notes. These audits establish locator integrity, not concept fit or textual provenance.

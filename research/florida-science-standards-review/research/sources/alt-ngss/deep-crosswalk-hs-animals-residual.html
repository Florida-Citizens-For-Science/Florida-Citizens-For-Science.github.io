Residual audit: unresolved Florida HS animal-biology benchmarks

Research date: October 22, 2026
Scope: The 97 SC.912.A.* rows marked unresolved at the start of the source-by-source pass. This is an audit snapshot. Sixteen of its strongest partial/carryover candidates were subsequently entered in the register, and later source checks added 21 more qualified links, including ten external-comparator links documented below.

Method and judgment labels

Each row was checked against the preserved Florida 2008 standards, surviving Florida 1996 high-school text, the available 1998/99 GLEs (K, 3–5, and 6–8 only), NGSS, and the indexed external-standards corpus. Matches require a specific concept in a source passage. General subject overlap (for example, both texts discussing animals, cells, or ecosystems) is not enough. GLEs below high-school grade are developmental context only.

  • Carryover: a specific concept is explicitly present in a predecessor; this does not establish copying or access.
  • Partial: a relevant concept is present, but the draft adds a material distinction, group, mechanism, or application.
  • No predecessor located: the screened corpus contains no specific support for the claimed detail. This is not evidence of novelty or omission from instruction.
  • Unresolved: the available evidence is too indirect to classify even as partial.

Counts and candidate register updates

The original 97-row audit classified 4 carryover, 40 partial, 52 no predecessor located, and 1 unresolved at review time. These are evidence-strength labels, not findings of borrowing, omission, or instructional divergence. A later source check entered four additional partial concept links in the register: SC.912.A.12.13 (disease and population limits), SC.912.A.14.12 (sexual reproduction/fertilization), SC.912.A.14.14 (egg-laying and animal life cycles), and SC.912.A.14.15 (reproductive success and competition). A further review entered seven medium-confidence links for SC.912.A.7.1, .7.5, .7.10, .7.13, .7.16, .7.23, and .7.27; the 2008 benchmark SC.912.L.15.7 explicitly asks for distinguishing characteristics of representative invertebrate phyla and chordate classes. The 2026 body-plan benchmarks match this broad same-grade classification/characteristics concept, while their specified symmetry, tissues, coverings, segmentation, habitat, and protective structures add detail not stated in the 2008 line. At that audit checkpoint the register had 49 unresolved SC.912.A.* rows. Subsequent source reviews reduced the current register count to 13; group-specific physiology, reproduction, and ecological roles remain unresolved where the cited predecessor is too general.

Most plausible row-level updates from this pass, subject to parent review: SC.912.A.2.2, .2.6, .3.5–.7, .5.1, .5.3, .7.12, .8.3, .9.3, .11.4, .12.3, .12.14, .14.4, .14.10, and .20.6. These sixteen initial updates are now in the register; the four additional later updates are listed above. Several are partial and must preserve the extra specificity listed above. The remaining “possible” entries indicate a weaker broad conceptual relation and are not recommended as automatic register edits.

Later external comparator links for homeostasis and adaptation

A focused check of South Carolina 2014 grade-6 animal standards added ten medium-confidence partial comparisons. Standard 6.L.4B.5 compares endothermic and ectothermic animals’ responses to environmental temperature; this supports only the temperature-response component of five grade-12 draft homeostasis benchmarks. Standards 6.L.4B.2–.3 address structural adaptations and animal responses such as hibernation, migration, grouping, and courtship; these support broad adaptation/behavior components in five grade-12 animal-class benchmarks. The draft’s fish/amphibian/reptile/bird/mammal specificity, aquatic/terrestrial habitats, physiological mechanisms, and water-balance content are not established by these standards. The South Carolina document is a later external comparator, not evidence of Florida drafting access or borrowing.

Coverage, shifts, and limits

The reviewed predecessor materials provide stronger support for general cell parts, cell functions, classification, reproduction, and ecosystem processes than for the draft’s fine-grained organelle mechanisms and group-specific zoology. The draft’s unresolved rows frequently name a particular animal phylum/class, internal mechanism, or ecological role for which the corpus contains only a broader principle. External standards yielded a small number of concrete comparisons (including centrioles/cytoskeleton, Planaria, marine-mammal insulation, teeth/diet, and invasive-species solutions). These support concept comparisons, not provenance claims.

Absence of a passage from the preserved corpus is not evidence that the content was newly introduced, omitted from earlier instruction, or borrowed from a particular outside source. This audit cannot establish authorship, access, drafting history, classroom order, instructional time, or student outcomes. The surviving 1996 text is broad; available GLEs stop at grade 8; and the external corpus is not exhaustive. The 2006 MA text, 2016 MA framework, California framework, South Carolina standards, NGSS, and Florida Citizens/FCS text are external comparators with different grade scopes and purposes, not interchangeable predecessors.

Parent review reconciliation

A subsequent direct review recorded eleven partial concept comparisons in the register. Four draft migration benchmarks for amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals (SC.912.A.9.6, .10.6, .11.7, .12.6) now link to NGSS HS-LS2-8, whose clarification names migrating among group behaviors and evaluates effects on survival and reproduction. The Florida rows add taxon-specific movement-impact analysis; no Florida 2008, 1996, or surviving GLE passage located that specific application. SC.912.A.12.4 now links partially to California 2006’s fish-gill/mammal-lung comparison (lines 2832–2833), which supports one mammal feature but not a comprehensive account. Four taxon-specific cycle rows (SC.912.A.9.5, .10.5, .11.6, .12.5) now link to Florida 2008 SC.912.L.17.10, which names water, carbon, and nitrogen biogeochemical cycles; the species-specific roles remain additional. These content links do not establish Florida workgroup access or wording provenance. Two later partial comparisons concern cell structure: SC.912.A.3.1 and .6.2 cite Massachusetts 2006 Biology 2.1 for cytoplasm/ribosome function, but that passage does not support the draft’s specific animal-cell features or free-versus-attached ribosome distinction. The SC.912.A.* unresolved count falls from 60 to 49; the remaining possible entries were not promoted where their source concepts were too general or did not match the draft’s distinguishing task.

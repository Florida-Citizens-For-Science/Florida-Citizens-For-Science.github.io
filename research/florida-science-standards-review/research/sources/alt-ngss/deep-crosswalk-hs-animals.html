Deep crosswalk: Florida high-school animal biology (strand A)

Research date: October 22, 2026
Scope: All grade 9–12 draft leaf benchmarks with IDs SC.912.A.*. This pass reviewed every unresolved row against the preserved Florida 2008 science standards, surviving Florida 1996 high-school standards, and 1998/99 GLE documents where grade placement was appropriate. It records supported concept correspondences; it does not establish drafting provenance or direct borrowing.

Result

The strand contains 221 benchmarks. At the start of this pass, 203 were unresolved and 18 already had explicit comparisons. The initial pass added 106 concept matches: 19 high-confidence and 87 medium-confidence, and assigned no divergence or non-divergent labels. At the end of that pass, 97 remained unresolved; absence of a specific predecessor passage was not treated as proof of novelty or omission. A subsequent residual source review entered additional medium/high-confidence matches in the register. A later South Carolina 2014 comparison added ten bounded external concept matches for animal temperature responses and structural/behavioral adaptations; these are later comparators, not Florida predecessors or provenance evidence. The current register has 26 high-confidence matches, 182 medium-confidence matches, and 13 unresolved rows in this strand. The current row list is in the residual audit.

All 106 new source anchors were checked against the source text using physical LF line numbering. Each cited 2008 benchmark ID exists, and the descriptive line is within three LF lines of its code; there were zero new-anchor errors. Earlier comparisons were not recounted as additions.

Source coverage and grade fit

Florida 2008 standards

The clearest same-grade predecessors are in the high-school life-science sequences:

  • SC.912.L.14.2–.3 addresses structure/function of plant and animal cell components and general plant/animal cell structure; .14.2 names selective membrane transport. These passages support shared component, membrane, and transport concepts. The 2026 sequence specifies organelles and animal-cell processes in much finer increments. The reviewed 2008 passages do not specifically identify several finer topics in the draft, including extracellular matrix components, peroxisomes, vesicle/endosome lumens, multiple nucleoli, or many sub-organelle processes.
  • SC.912.L.14.5 addresses evidence for eukaryotic-cell endosymbiosis, supporting the mitochondrion/eukaryotic-origin connection but not other mitochondrial comparisons.
  • SC.912.L.14.29–.46 and .49–.50 cover human endocrine, nervous, reproductive, cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, and vertebrate sensory/integumentary systems. They support selected shared physiological processes. The 2026 strand reframes these around animal groups, comparative adaptations, and animal-wide integration.
  • SC.912.L.15.6–.7 covers distinguishing characteristics of kingdoms, vertebrate and representative invertebrate phyla, and chordate classes. This directly supports named-group classification. It does not enumerate the draft’s detailed anatomy, systems, reproduction, or habitat-specific adaptations for each group.
  • SC.912.L.16.14–.17 treats mitosis, meiosis, haploid gametes, and the relationship between cell division, sexual/asexual reproduction, and genetic variation. It grounds some reproduction concepts but not many animal-specific modes, mating strategies, egg features, and life-history comparisons.
  • SC.912.L.17.1–.10 covers population characteristics, aquatic distribution and processes, succession, growth/carrying capacity, organism relationships, freshwater/marine/terrestrial systems, biodiversity loss, trophic energy transfer, and water/carbon/nitrogen cycles. It supports these shared ecological concepts without the 2026 taxon-by-taxon detail. SC.912.L.17.17 evaluates environmental-protection methods, but does not specify invasive-animal control mechanisms.
  • SC.912.L.18.8 identifies the reactants, products, and functions of aerobic and anaerobic cellular respiration. It supports the repeated cellular-respiration concept in the animal-physiology sequence; the 2026 draft extends it as an explanatory link among movement, growth, repair, organ activity, and integrated animal systems.

The 2026 text often has a clear predecessor concept but adds substantial detail. Medium-confidence comparisons indicate partial scope continuity; they do not imply that 2008 required every named detail.

Surviving Florida 1996 high-school standards

The 1996 high-school section is broader and less granular than the 2008 benchmarks. SC.F.1.4 describes structure/function patterns in living things and includes biochemical body processes, adapted body structures, cell membranes, cell signaling, and response to stimuli. SC.F.2.4 explicitly addresses sexual/asexual reproduction and their genetic advantages/disadvantages, plus DNA, mutation, selection, and adaptation. SC.G.1.4 treats interdependence, ecosystem energy flow, and cycling. These statements reinforce broad concept continuity for cell biology, physiology, reproduction, and ecology, but do not substantiate the draft’s specific organelle or taxon-level content. Where useful, the register keeps the more precise 2008 benchmark locator.

1998/99 Grade-Level Expectations

The preserved GLE text files cover K, grades 3–5, and grades 6–8; this corpus contains no grade 9–12 GLE file. The middle-school GLEs can indicate earlier exposure, but are not same-grade predecessors and were not used to label an HS row as matched. The included 1998 science-review material is not a substitute for missing high-school GLE benchmark text.

Coverage shifts visible in the draft

  1. More granular cell biology. The 2008 cell standards group component structure/function and membrane transport. The 2026 strand divides instruction into many organelle, substructure, transport, signaling, and organelle-specific leaves. Unmatched detail remains unresolved rather than being called divergent.
  2. Taxon-by-taxon natural history. The 2008 classification benchmark asks for representative phyla and chordate classes. The 2026 draft gives separate leaves to sponges, cnidarians, worms, mollusks, arthropods, echinoderms, and each vertebrate class, then repeats anatomy, physiology, reproduction, adaptation, and ecology by group. This describes a finer topic layout; it does not establish instructional time.
  3. Animal-specific ecology. The 2008 standards state common population, interaction, trophic, ecosystem, and cycle principles. The draft reapplies these across fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals, and invasive animals, and adds animal-mediated roles such as keystone predators, ecosystem engineers, nutrient redistribution, and disease impacts. General principles map where supported; group-specific mechanisms remain unresolved without a specific predecessor passage.
  4. Physiology linked repeatedly to respiration. The draft casts many animal systems in terms of energy use and cellular respiration. The 2008 standards cover respiratory physiology and cellular respiration but do not consistently connect respiration to the draft’s detailed animal-system and behavioral claims.

These observations describe scope and wording structure only. Benchmark-register order does not prove classroom pacing or instructional time.

Method and confidence

Every unresolved A row was reviewed individually. A match was added only where a predecessor statement supports a recognizable shared concept, not merely because both texts concern animals or ecology. High confidence denotes a directly named concept or close concept-level correspondence. Medium confidence denotes a supported but partial correspondence where the draft adds taxon-specific framing, detail, or application. Confidence does not assert verbatim copying. Where no specific grade-appropriate passage supported a match, the row remains unresolved; absence was not treated as divergence.

Limits

  • The preserved corpus may omit unpublished drafts, meeting materials, alternate versions, or source files. A match does not establish what the working group consulted.
  • Shared concepts do not establish textual provenance, authorship, or timing of access.
  • The high-level 1996 statements and lower-grade 1998/99 GLEs do not support benchmark-level equivalence for every HS detail.
  • “Unresolved” means this corpus and this pass do not establish a sufficiently specific grade-appropriate predecessor. It does not mean unique, omitted, or absent from instruction.
  • This strand crosswalk cannot establish grade sequencing, calendar placement, instructional time, or retention effects.

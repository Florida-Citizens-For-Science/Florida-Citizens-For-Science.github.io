Deep crosswalk pass: high-school Earth and space systems

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: 45 previously unresolved grade 9–12 PE (planet Earth) benchmarks, compared directly with Florida 2008, surviving Florida 1996 standards, and the available 1998/99 GLE materials.

Results

The initial pass added 24 concept-level mappings (6 high-confidence and 18 medium-confidence) and individually re-reviewed 21 rows. A subsequent targeted review linked the seven SC.912.PE.16.1–.7 anthropogenic Earth-system benchmarks to the more specific 2008 climate, biodiversity, cycle, erosion, and sustainability predecessors cited in the register. The historical-source pass recorded 34 sourced concept-level mappings across this high-school Earth/space strand, including seven anthropogenic Earth-system links and three grade-7-to-high-school geologic-time links. The 18 rows initially left without a specific Florida 2008/1996/GLE predecessor were checked against NGSS: 11 received external NGSS concept matches, while 7 remained unresolved against the full local corpus. A subsequent source recheck found Florida 2008 SC.6.E.7.9 directly relevant to three atmospheric benchmarks, now recorded in the register as concept matches, bringing the strand’s historical Florida crosswalk to 41 sourced concept links. A review of the uncertainty/evidence benchmark also found general Florida 2008 Nature of Science predecessors, recorded as a medium-confidence concept match. A final crosscheck linked the three geomagnetic/navigation rows to general electromagnetism, atmospheric/radiation protection, and compass-direction benchmarks, respectively. Those links preserve the added Earth-specific mechanisms and applications. The NGSS links clarify cross-standard content relationships; they do not establish source use or wording provenance.

The mappings trace broad continuity in Earth structure and surface change, plate tectonics, Earth spheres, energy/matter cycles, climate-system interactions, fossils/geologic change, water shaping the surface, and severe-weather prediction. The strongest direct predecessors are Florida 2008 SC.912.E.6.1–.4 (Earth layers, surface processes, plate tectonics and geologic features), SC.912.E.7.1–.5 (biogeochemical cycles, Earth-system interactions, climate, and weather prediction), SC.912.E.7.9 (severe-weather formation), and SC.912.L.15.1 (fossil evidence for evolutionary change). Florida 1996 high-school SC.D.1.4 independently describes interactions among Earth spheres, plate tectonics, climatic patterns, and change over geologic time.

All seven rows that were unresolved after the first historical/external screen now have concept-level connections in the local corpus. The added Florida links cover the atmosphere’s protective role and structure; general evaluation of evidence/inference; motion-generated magnetic fields; planetary protection from radiation; and compass-based direction finding. These are partial predecessor concepts, not matches for the draft’s full Earth-specific mechanisms or tasks. Nearby broad Earth-science concepts were not treated as proof of a specific predecessor. The other historically unmatched rows have the external NGSS comparisons below; their full Florida-predecessor status remains visible in the individual notes and external comparison distinction.

Florida predecessor recheck: atmosphere content

Florida 2008 SC.6.E.7.9 states that atmospheric composition and structure protect life and insulate the planet (local text lines 2038–2040). This passage supports specific concept-level continuity for three current high-school benchmarks, with the draft’s additional scope retained in the register:

2026 benchmark Florida 2008 connection Added detail and placement change
SC.912.PE.5.1 Atmospheric composition The draft names atmospheric gases and their relative proportions and explains gravitational retention; 2008 does not state the gravity mechanism. Grade 6 to high school. Medium-confidence partial concept match.
SC.912.PE.5.2 Atmospheric structure The draft differentiates five layers by temperature and composition using vertical profiles; the predecessor does not name layers or these analysis tasks. Grade 6 to high school. Medium-confidence partial concept match.
SC.912.PE.5.4 Atmosphere protects life The draft specifies ozone/UV absorption, harmful solar/cosmic radiation, and meteoroid breakup. Grade 6 to high school. High-confidence concept match for the protective role; mechanisms and examples are additions.

These links reduce the Florida-historically-unmatched rows by three. They do not show that the 2026 language was copied from 2008; the older source is a concise content predecessor, and the draft substantially elaborates the mechanism and assessment task.

SC.912.PE.12.6 also now has a medium-confidence Florida concept link through 2008 Nature of Science benchmarks: SC.912.N.1.3 evaluates scientific claim strength/usefulness through argumentation and alternatives; SC.912.N.1.6 addresses drawing inferences from observations; and SC.912.N.2.4 describes knowledge as robust yet open to change with new investigations (local text lines 2661–2678, 2714–2717). The 2026 draft applies these operations specifically to inaccessible Earth-interior evidence and uncertainty. It pairs limitations with independent evidence sources and revision; that scope is not a generalized claim that scientific methods are inadequate.

2026 benchmark NGSS comparison Scope difference retained in the register
SC.912.PE.2.3 HS-ESS2-3 describes radial Earth layers by density and cites high-pressure laboratory experiments for composition (AllTopic p.96; text lines 5439–5444). NGSS does not state the full draft task linking density, temperature, and pressure to Earth-material behavior.
SC.912.PE.9.1 The HS-ESS2-3 Earth-science DCI says radioactive decay generates energy that drives mantle convection (p.96; lines 5474–5482). The draft also identifies residual heat from Earth’s formation and adds historical context.
SC.912.PE.9.2 HS-ESS2-3 directly connects thermal convection inside Earth to mantle movement and plate tectonics (p.96; lines 5439–5444, 5474–5482). The draft adds a temperature/density demonstration and explicit heat-transfer relationships.
SC.912.PE.12.3 HS-ESS2-3 uses seismic structure, magnetic-field changes, and high-pressure experiments as evidence about Earth’s interior (p.96; lines 5439–5444, 5470–5478). The draft also specifies gravity and heat-flow measurements and asks students to analyze quantitative relationships.
SC.912.PE.12.4 HS-ESS2-3 asks students to develop an evidence-based model of Earth’s interior, including one- and three-dimensional models (p.96; lines 5439–5444, 5470–5478). The draft adds named physical, mathematical, and computer models; model revision; and evaluation against observed phenomena.
SC.912.PE.12.5 HS-ESS2-3 cites multiple evidence types—seismic structure, magnetic-field records, and high-pressure composition experiments—for an interior model (p.96; lines 5439–5444, 5470–5478). The draft makes explicit synthesis across seismic, geochemical, and geophysical data and explains that no single evidence type is sufficient.
SC.912.PE.15.2 MS-ESS3-2 uses mapped hazard history and hazard locations to forecast events (AllTopic p.59; text lines 3528–3534). The draft adds global/regional pattern comparison across tectonic, climate, and geographic factors at high school.
SC.912.PE.15.3 MS-ESS3-2 names hazard magnitudes and frequencies among relevant data (p.59; lines 3528–3534). The draft asks students to evaluate impacts across spatial/temporal scales and includes exposure of people, infrastructure, and biological communities.
SC.912.PE.15.4 MS-ESS3-2 connects forecast likelihood from hazard data to mitigation decisions (p.59; lines 3528–3534). It does not explicitly define risk through likelihood, exposure, and vulnerability or require probability/risk-model evaluation as the draft does.
SC.912.PE.15.6 MS-ESS3-2 links analysis of natural hazards with technologies to mitigate effects (p.59; lines 3528–3534). The draft evaluates Earth-system-based strategies and their effectiveness and limitations.
SC.912.PE.15.7 MS-ESS3-2 links hazard data, forecasting, monitoring, and mitigation (p.59; lines 3528–3534). The draft adds synthesis of interactions among Earth systems and human activity and changing risk over time.

Method and limits

Each row was checked against the complete relevant 2008 Earth/space and life-science passages, 1996 high-school Earth-science text, and surviving GLE extracts. The 18 historical residual rows were subsequently screened against NGSS as documented above. Positive matches are concept-level comparisons, not wording provenance. Some notes explicitly state the added method or detail in the draft that the older benchmark does not name. No divergence or non-divergent finding was inferred from source absence. The GLE corpus has no surviving high-school GLE text, so no high-school GLE match or omission is inferred.

This is a strand-level deep pass, not completion of all 1,501 comparisons. See the register and legacy crosswalk supplement for row evidence and limitations.

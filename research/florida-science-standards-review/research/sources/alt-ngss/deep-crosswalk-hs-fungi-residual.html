Residual crosswalk: unresolved 2026 high-school fungi benchmarks

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: An initial audit of 23 SC.912.FG.* leaf benchmarks marked “unresolved vs research corpus” at the time of that review. Later follow-up sections document register updates; the live status is in the shared benchmark register and main report.

Finding

At the start of the initial audit, the 52 high-school fungi leaves included 29 medium-confidence concept matches and 23 unresolved rows. The initial table accounts for those 23 IDs. “Unresolved” means that the reviewed local corpus does not establish a sufficiently specific predecessor or source relationship. It does not mean the content is scientifically novel, absent from all earlier instruction, copied from an outside source, or deliberately added for a particular purpose. Later source checks reduced the unresolved count in stages; the current register has one unresolved fungi benchmark (SC.912.FG.3.3).

Florida 2008 gives a broad high-school expectation to relate fungal structures to their functions (SC.912.L.14.7, source text LF line 3202), and includes separate, broader cell-organelle, cellular-process, reproduction, and ecology benchmarks. Those anchors already support concept-level matches elsewhere in this strand. They do not state most of the fine-grained fungal cell, organelle, reproduction, or physiology tasks listed below. The 1996 Florida high-school text covers general cell regulation and sexual/asexual reproduction and broad ecosystem concepts, not these specific fungal mechanisms. The surviving Florida 1998/99 GLEs in the corpus are grades K–8, so there is no same-grade high-school GLE to compare; selected middle-grade GLE concepts are noted where relevant.

Crosswalk implications and provenance limits

The clearest content-level continuity for these rows is in general cell biology, sexual/asexual reproduction, ecological succession, environmental limits, and organism responses. The draft’s fungal-specific formulations, including nucleolus and organelle roles, fungal reproductive structures, extracellular digestion, and the linkage between fungal adaptations and persistence or recovery, are more specific than the located Florida predecessor wording. That is a comparison of stated benchmark scope; it does not establish that these are newly invented ideas or that they were sourced from any particular document.

Relevant external parallels in the inventoried corpus are limited but useful for follow-up: NGSS LS2.A explicitly names fungi as decomposers and the return of materials to soil (ngss-all-topic-standards.txt, LF 1654–1657); Massachusetts 2016 5-LS2-1 similarly names fungi and material recycling (LF 2050–2054); South Carolina 2014 has more fungal-specific grade 6 content on heterotrophy/external absorption, fruiting structures, and environmental responses (LF 2145–2162). Its grade 7 cell benchmark describes cytoplasm, cell wall, nucleus, mitochondria, and vacuoles but scopes those examples to plant, animal, and bacterial cells (LF 2385–2387). Massachusetts 8.MS-LS3-2 covers advantages/disadvantages of sexual and asexual reproduction without a fungi-specific scope (LF 2815–2824). These establish subject-matter parallels worth retaining in corpus search, not wording provenance, access, or authorship.

Florida’s surviving 1998/99 GLEs include middle-grade general cell, reproduction, and soil/decomposition concepts (locators in the table), but no high-school GLE volume was found in this corpus. Therefore they cannot support same-grade sequence conclusions for these grade 9–12 draft rows. The 2026 draft pages give grade-band placement but not a within-year order, so this audit does not infer semester timing or instructional sequence from page order.

The source comparison does not identify a specific candidate external document for these 23 detailed rows, and no matching textual phrase was established here. To test provenance, the corpus would need additional candidate source documents with text and metadata. Even a wording match would need document history and process records to assess when material entered the draft and by whom; the standards text alone cannot establish access or intent.

Register update after follow-up source check

A later source-by-source review established medium-confidence concept continuity for two of the 23 rows audited above. Florida 1996 SC.F.2.4.1 explicitly covers sexual and asexual reproduction and their differing genetic advantages/disadvantages; Florida 2008 SC.912.L.16.14–.17 covers binary fission, mitosis, meiosis, and genetic variation; the grade 7 GLE SC.F.2.3.1 names differences and advantages of both modes in plants and animals. The register records these links for SC.912.FG.8.1 and .8.2. The audit’s historical scope and “23 unresolved” table remain a record of the earlier review; 21 rows remained unresolved at that checkpoint. These concept matches do not establish textual borrowing or drafting access.

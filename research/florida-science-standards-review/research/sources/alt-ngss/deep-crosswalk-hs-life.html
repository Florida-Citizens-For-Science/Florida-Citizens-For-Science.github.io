Deep crosswalk pass: selected grade 9–12 strands

Scope and method

This pass reviewed the 221 rows that were marked unresolved when work began in the grade 9–12 strands CB, HB, SL, ASL, EL, ES, and EE. Each draft benchmark was compared directly with the preserved grade 9–12 Florida 2008 standards and the grade 9–12 Florida Sunshine State Standards (1996), including full benchmark language and standard descriptions where a short label could conceal a related concept. An anchor was added only when a specific predecessor concept was present. The match notes distinguish that concept from new context, calculation, integration, or level of detail. Similar vocabulary or a broad science relationship alone did not qualify.

The preserved 1998/99 GLE files are for K, grades 3–5, and grades 6–8; there is no high-school GLE source in this corpus. They therefore cannot establish a high-school predecessor and were not cited as such. A matching 2008/1996 concept supports continuity only; it does not establish copying, access, or drafting provenance. The pass does not turn retrieval absence into a divergence finding.

The direct comparisons show substantial concept continuity. Examples include cell theory and membrane transport (2008 SC.912.L.14.1–3); photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and ATP (2008 SC.912.L.18.5–10); DNA replication, mutation, gene expression, mitosis, meiosis, and inheritance (SC.912.L.16.1–17); human nervous, endocrine, respiratory, digestive, urinary, musculoskeletal, and immune systems (SC.912.L.14.11–52); and population, ecosystem, and environmental processes (SC.912.L.17.1–20). The 1996 9–12 text has broad counterparts in Processes of Life (SC.F.1.4, SC.F.2.4), ecological relationships and resources (SC.G.1.4, SC.G.2.4), Earth processes (SC.D.1.4), and energy (SC.B.1.4). Row-level anchors and qualified match notes are in 2026-benchmark-register.csv.

The most visible structural change in this slice is the draft’s use of parallel energy strands for Earth, space, and life contexts, alongside far more granular cell and human-body sequences. Many underlying concepts already occur in 2008 and 1996, while named pathway stages, individual cell-cycle phases, specialized calculations, and cross-system applications are often draft-specific elaborations rather than separately stated predecessor benchmarks. The register preserves this distinction instead of treating every elaboration as a new concept.

The initial pass left 34 rows unresolved, including cellular signaling (CB.9), microbiome functions (HB.11), chemosynthesis (CB.2.7–.8), cell-size/surface-area constraints (CB.4.1–.2), named cell-cycle subphases, several detailed cancer mechanisms, and some applied-life-science/career statements. The shared register has since changed; its current status for these seven strands is 16 unresolved: 9 CB, 5 HB, and 2 ASL rows. The remaining CB IDs are SC.912.CB.2.7, .4.1–.4.2, and .9.2, .9.6–.9.10; the other IDs are SC.912.HB.11.1–.11.5 and SC.912.ASL.1.1, .5.1. The other reviewed SL, EL, ES, and EE rows currently have explicit comparisons. These unresolved rows are source-level review leads, not confirmed omissions or evidence of outside provenance. This pass addresses only the seven listed strands and makes no claim about grade placement within a school year; the 912 grade band and benchmark order do not supply calendar pacing information.

Audit trail

All new 2008 anchors point to sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt; where applicable, additional 1996 anchors point to sources/1996/florida-1996-science-912.txt. A source-file-and-line audit was run against every new match: 187 draft rows checked, zero bad or missing cited source lines. The unchanged unresolved rows retain no inferred predecessor anchor.

Deep crosswalk pass: high-school matter and chemistry

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: 41 previously unresolved 2026 high-school M (matter) benchmarks, compared directly with the local Florida 2008 standards, the surviving 1996 high-school standards, and the available 1998/99 GLE files.

Results

This direct review added 30 concept-level predecessor mappings: 7 high-confidence and 23 medium-confidence. At this review checkpoint, eleven rows remained unresolved because the available predecessor text did not state the specific task or method. Later source-specific checks resolved all eleven SC.912.M.* rows to supported partial or other register classifications; the current unresolved count for this strand is zero. The added mappings are recorded in the all-ID register, with source paths, exact local line ranges, and row-level scope notes. No row is labeled divergent or unchanged based on absence from a source.

High-confidence continuity includes atom structure; formula composition; selected functional groups; physical versus chemical change; energy transfer in reactions; oxidation-reduction; and mole/conservation calculations. Medium-confidence matches capture broader predecessor concepts such as bonding, periodic trends, phase changes, gases, acids/bases, equilibrium, reaction rates, and chemical heat changes. In each of those, the 2026 draft either specifies a particular mechanism, representation, calculation, or application not named in the 2008 predecessor.

The strongest direct 2008 comparison is high-school Physical Science Standard 8, whose benchmarks cover atom structure and periodic properties, bonding forces, formula interpretation, reaction types, mole-based calculations, redox, functional groups, and acid/base concentration. Standards 10 and 12 add energy in chemical processes, heat, phase changes, ideal-gas behavior, reaction rates, and dynamic equilibrium. The 1996 high-school Nature of Matter standards independently establish earlier continuity for electron configuration, bonding, phase changes, reaction-rate factors, atomic theory, and periodic families. These are conceptual predecessors, not evidence that the 2026 writers consulted or copied those documents.

The eleven initially unresolved task-specific rows covered hydrocarbon-family naming; IUPAC/isomer work; Lewis/VSEPR dipole-vector polarity; phase diagrams and critical/triple points; solution-concentration calculations; integrated Earth/space matter contexts; buffer design; titration analysis; equilibrium-constant/thermodynamics integration; half-reaction balancing; and galvanic-cell analysis. These historical leads were subsequently reviewed and updated in the current register. Other unproven chemistry detail remains bounded by row notes and should not be generalized into a claim of novelty or omission from instruction.

Method and limits

Each of the 41 previously unresolved high-school M leaves was checked against the full 2008 high-school chemistry and related physics text, relevant 1996 high-school matter/energy text, and surviving GLE extracts. For positive mappings, the register cites the benchmark text and physical extract line range. For partial parallels, the note identifies which component is supported and which current-draft detail goes beyond the older wording. The remaining 11 were individually checked against those same source areas and kept unresolved where the closest match was only a broad topic. The 1998/99 GLE corpus has no surviving high-school GLE, so no high-school GLE predecessor is inferred.

This is one strand-level direct review, not completion of the full 1,512-row crosswalk. It does not turn other unresolved rows into omissions by default. See the 2026 benchmark register for row evidence and the legacy crosswalk supplement for prior cross-band work and retrieval limitations.

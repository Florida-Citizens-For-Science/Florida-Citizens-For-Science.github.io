Deep crosswalk: unresolved high-school microorganisms benchmarks

Scope and method

This pass audits the 30 grade 9–12 SC.912.MO rows that remain marked “unresolved vs research corpus” in 2026-benchmark-register.csv at the time of review. The register has 50 high-school microorganisms benchmarks overall; 20 already have concept-level predecessor links and are not re-adjudicated here. I checked each of the 30 unresolved statements against the Florida 2008 high-school standards, the 1996 high-school standards, surviving Florida 1998–99 GLE files, NGSS/NRC material, and the locally preserved alternative-jurisdiction sources (Massachusetts 2006 and South Carolina 2014). Draft wording and its benchmark-level clarifications/examples were checked in sources/2026/fl-912.txt at the line locators below.

Judgments use four labels: carryover means a substantially matching concept is explicitly present in a prior Florida benchmark; partial means a predecessor supports only part of the concept or a broader neighboring concept; no prior concept located means the checked prior Florida corpus has no sufficiently specific concept; unresolved means available material is too incomplete or broad to decide. These labels concern content continuity, not textual provenance. External standards are treated as comparators or possible content sources; without documentary evidence of access or a distinctive wording match, they do not establish borrowing.

The corpus contains 1998–99 Florida GLEs only for kindergarten, grades 3–5, and grades 6–8. There is no high-school GLE file. The 1996 grade 9–12 text is present, but it states broad life-science and ecosystem concepts rather than the microbial mechanisms covered by this draft strand. An absent hit in those files is not proof that a concept was never taught or discussed elsewhere.

These broad environmental benchmarks do not specify microbial populations, microbial metabolism, or microbial-process management. 1996 SC.G.2.4.5–.6 discusses human activities and natural-system risks (florida-1996-science-912.txt:141–150) but not microbes. Partial for anthropogenic environmental change and sustainability; no prior concept located for the microbial causal mechanisms and strategies.

The only clear high-school microbial concepts in the 2008 Florida corpus are in the broader adjacent benchmarks listed above. The direct microbial text is sparse: viruses as infectious agents; virus/bacteria genetic transfer; binary fission; and general ecology/cycles. The more granular MO strand is therefore a substantial expansion of specificity. This finding is about coverage in the checked standards files; it does not establish when, where, or from whom the added content was drafted.

Limits and next research leads

  1. The 1998–99 high-school GLE is absent. Locate an authentic grade 9–12 GLE edition or contemporaneous Florida curriculum maps before treating a concept as a new grade-band addition.
  2. The 1996 and 2008 standards are authoritative content baselines, not complete records of all materials available to the 2026 writing group. Ancillary curriculum frameworks, course descriptions, and science course maps could add relevant history.
  3. The external comparator sample is limited to locally preserved NGSS/NRC, Massachusetts 2006, and South Carolina 2014 files. Matching concepts do not prove access, authorship, or lifting. The Florida 2008 process’ mention of Massachusetts/South Carolina is not evidence that the later draft group used those documents.
  4. Benchmark-level order within the draft is not a school-year pacing calendar. This file makes no claim that a benchmark’s placement would de-emphasize or prioritize it in classroom time.
  5. A strong provenance conclusion would need source versions available before the relevant drafting dates, exact phrase/context comparison, document metadata and version history where available, and a traceable path showing access. No sectarian or denialist framing signal was identified by this microbiology-content audit; it was not designed as a broad rhetoric audit of every clarification.

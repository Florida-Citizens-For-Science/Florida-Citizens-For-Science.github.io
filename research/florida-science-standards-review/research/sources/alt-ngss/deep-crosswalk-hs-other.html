Deep crosswalk pass: high-school strands ASL, CB, EN, FG, MO, NT, and SP

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: The 2026 draft’s previously unresolved grade 9–12 leaves in ASL (applied science of life), CB (cellular biology), EN (energy), FG (fungi), MO (microorganisms), NT (nutrition), and SP (space science). The register was reloaded immediately before writing the row updates; only still-unresolved grade 9–12 rows in these strands were changed.

Results

At the start of this pass, the specified strands contained 202 previously unresolved rows: ASL 3, CB 26, EN 38, FG 49, MO 47, NT 19, and SP 20. It added 105 medium-confidence concept-level mappings and one divergence judgment, each with one or more Florida 2008 and/or 1996 passage anchors in the benchmark register. The remaining 96 rows stay unresolved: ASL 3, CB 16, EN 2, FG 23, MO 30, NT 19, and SP 3. These counts describe this bounded set of rows and are not corpus-wide match rates.

The strongest direct carryovers are energy forms, work/power, heat transfer, temperature, entropy and efficiency, electrical relationships, photosynthesis, cell-cycle/mitosis and meiosis, cancer-related uncontrolled growth, fungal structure/function, fungi and microorganisms in ecosystem processes, microbe genetic transfer and binary fission, universal gravitation, stellar equilibrium and development, galaxy arrangements, astronomical observation tools, and broad cosmology/modeling. The draft often adds finer calculation tasks, named cell-cycle phases, organism-specific applications, or more integrated system reasoning than the older benchmark states. The row notes make these limits explicit.

At the time of this pass, all 19 high-school NT rows were unresolved. A later dedicated nutrition review assigned 8 medium-confidence concept matches for nutrient/body-process and evidence-evaluation concepts, 10 divergence findings for processing categories, regulatory definitions, labels, cost/density comparisons, and processed-food health claims, and left 1 comparison unresolved (hydration physiology). The three unresolved ASL rows still state broad applied-life-science or career outcomes without a specific older high-school benchmark.

Method and limits

For every in-scope unresolved row, the draft leaf was compared directly with the relevant grade 9–12 language in Florida’s 2008 standards and the surviving 1996 Florida high-school standards. A mapping was added only when an identifiable predecessor benchmark stated the same underlying scientific concept. Notes distinguish that continuity from finer detail or application in the draft. Similar vocabulary and a broad topical relationship were not enough; a match is not evidence of textual borrowing, access, or authorship. No divergence or non-divergent status was inferred from missing predecessor language.

The surviving Florida 1998/99 Grade Level Expectations in this corpus cover kindergarten and grades 3–5 and 6–8; there is no grade 9–12 GLE document here. They therefore cannot establish same-grade high-school continuity or omission. For SC.912.EN.6.4, the grades 6–8 GLE says energy conversions are not 100% efficient (SC.B.1.3.4, local text lines 271–281), but it does not specify pressure–volume diagrams. The register records the high-school 1996 and 2008 predecessors and the task-specific divergence.

Rows left unresolved include detailed fungal and microbial cell structures, named fungal taxonomic subdivisions, microbial metabolic strategies and disease ecology, detailed cell signaling, most nutrition claims, energy-grid operations and storage methods, and specialized astronomy topics such as galaxy-matter mapping and near-Earth hazards. A later review linked the space-exploration trade-off benchmark to earlier Florida standards on future exploration and its broad economic/cultural effects; the draft’s explicit multi-factor trade-off framework remains additional. These are review gaps, not confirmed omissions. The pressure–volume-diagram row is now classified as a task-specific divergence from earlier work/thermodynamics content.

Thermodynamic diagrams and work

Draft SC.912.EN.6.4 (draft PDF p.17) asks students to interpret pressure–volume diagrams, relate work to the area under the curve, and identify four thermodynamic process types qualitatively. Florida 2008 SC.912.P.10.3 asks students to compare work and power, and .10.7 connects entropy to the efficiency of converting energy to work (local standards text lines 3022–3039). The 1996 high-school energy standard SC.B.1.4 expectations 6–7 relates the first law to work and heat transfer and notes that usable energy decreases (local text lines 16 and 36–41). The 1998/99 grades 6–8 GLE likewise describes energy conversions and efficiency (SC.B.1.3.4, lines 217 and 271–281). These provide a specific content predecessor; pressure–volume graph interpretation and naming of isobaric, isochoric, isothermal, and adiabatic processes are additional task-level specificity in the draft. The grade band remains high school. This row is now diverges for task specificity and graphical representation, not for a new thermodynamics subject area.

External comparison note: DNA replication regulation

At the time of the original comparison, 16 CB rows remained unresolved against the Florida 2008/1996/GLE predecessor corpus; they were not all absent from every external comparator. One useful parallel appears in the South Carolina 2014 Biology 1 framework: H.B.4D (printed p.77 / PDF p.81) says DNA replication is tightly regulated and highly accurate, while the Florida 2026 SC.912.CB.6.3 names DNA-polymerase proofreading, post-replication repair, residual errors, and evidence of repair effectiveness. A later Florida crosswalk records 2008 SC.912.L.16.3 as a partial concept link for DNA replication, but it does not describe regulation by proofreading/repair. The South Carolina passage remains a content-level analogue, not distinctive evidence of a text lift; it postdates the 2008 Florida standards and cannot explain their wording.

Subsequent partial cell-signaling and replication links

A focused follow-up added three medium-confidence links to Florida 2008. SC.912.CB.6.3 is linked to SC.912.L.16.3 for the basic DNA-replication process; regulation, proofreading, repair, and error-evidence tasks remain additional. SC.912.CB.9.4 is linked to SC.912.L.14.21 and .14.23 only for its synaptic signaling example; the general paracrine mechanism remains more specific. SC.912.CB.9.5 is linked to SC.912.L.14.28–.31 for endocrine control and hormone mechanisms; the draft’s pathway modeling and evidence-evaluation tasks remain additional. These concepts are grade-band continuous but do not establish wording provenance or borrowing. Later reviews reduced the current unresolved CB count from 13 to 9.

Citation audit

Every new anchor points to sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt or sources/1996/florida-1996-science-912.txt with a physical LF line number. Citation line numbers were checked against the text files using LF-delimited lines (so page form-feed characters do not shift the count). All 105 mapped rows have a predecessor code and a cited line containing that code; no malformed or missing cited lines were found.

Residual K–5 crosswalk review

Scope

I re-read the current benchmark register and screened its 44 K–5 entries marked unresolved vs research corpus against the preserved Florida 1996 and 1998–99 materials, adopted 2008 standards, NGSS, Massachusetts 2016, and South Carolina 2014 corpus. The following candidates are additional concept-level links worth recording. The listed SC.4.SL.1.2 candidate corrects an initial table typo (.1.4 was already mapped); all 21 benchmark IDs below were unresolved at the start of this review and have now been merged as concept matches. They are not findings of textual borrowing, authorship, or access. Grade placement is noted where the comparison comes from another grade band. A partial subject match does not establish that the complete draft task is represented.

All locators below are physical LF line numbers in the named local text files. The proposed anchors were checked against those lines. Source identifiers are shown where the source has a benchmark identifier; NGSS DCI text without a single PE locator is identified as such.

Follow-up nutrition review

Three additional nutrition/resource rows now have medium-confidence concept links to historical Florida content. The current standards add age-specific applications and broader dimensions; the links do not establish that each complete task was in the older curriculum.

Draft benchmark Historical source and locator Current crosswalk judgment
SC.2.NT.1.5 — identify ways people can reduce food waste 1998/99 GLE SC.D.2.2.1, grades 3–5 expectations, local text lines 481–503; 2008 SC.4.E.6.3, local text lines 1503–1504. The GLE explicitly asks students to reuse, recycle, and reduce natural-resource use; 2008 identifies natural resources as renewable or nonrenewable. The draft applies reduction to food waste in grade 2, earlier than the GLE expectation. Neither predecessor names food waste or gives food-specific reduction strategies, which remain new in the reviewed Florida corpus.
SC.3.NT.1.4 — identify carbohydrates, proteins, and fats as nutrients 2008 SC.912.L.18.1–.4, local standards text lines 3636–3651; 1998/99 GLE SC.B.1.1.5, grades 1–2 expectations, local text lines 177–189. The older GLE asks about nutritional value of foods and classifies food groups in grades 1–2; 2008 names carbohydrate, lipid, and protein categories/functions at high school. The draft introduces nutrient categories and their basic roles in grade 3, a strong concept match with shifted grade and more food-composition application.
SC.5.NT.1.5 — explain how different types of food influence nutrition 1998/99 GLE SC.B.1.1.5, grades 1–2 expectations, local text lines 177–189. The GLE names the nutritional value of fruit, cereals, dairy, and meat and asks students to classify food groups. The draft revisits food type and nutrition in grade 5, adding processing, availability, agriculture/transport/access, and effects over time. Those additions are not specified in the GLE.

Remaining concepts without a sufficiently specific counterpart

  • One K–5 benchmark remains unresolved in the current register: the human food-supply-chain task in SC.2.NT.1.4. SC.3.NT.1.5 and SC.5.NT.1.4 now have medium-confidence partial links to earlier food/nutrition expectations, while preparation-related nutrient change and long-term body-effect tasks remain draft-specific in the located corpus. Fungal reproduction has a medium-confidence, partial concept link, while its environment-comparison and fungal-spore details remain untraced. Five already-mapped benchmarks (SC.1.FG.1.1, SC.3.CB.1.1, SC.5.HB.1.2, SC.4.FG.1.1, SC.5.MO.1.1) also have more specific sub-concepts not covered by their partial predecessor links. Medium-confidence links establish concept continuity only; they do not make the new instrument operations, nutrition applications, or other details historical carryovers.

  • Nutrition and food systems: SC.2.NT.1.4 remains unresolved; no predecessor specifies the human food supply chain. SC.3.NT.1.5 and SC.5.NT.1.4 have partial nutrition concept matches, but the specific preparation-related nutrient-change and long-term body-effect tasks are not stated in the located corpus.

  • The specific fungal spread and environmental comparison task in SC.5.FG.1.1 remains unsupported, although the register now records partial concept continuity for reproduction. Florida 1996 SC.F.2.4 expectation 1 addresses mechanisms and genetic advantages/disadvantages of sexual and asexual reproduction (lines 149–152); 2008 SC.912.L.16.14–.17 addresses cell division, meiosis, spores, and sexual/asexual reproduction (lines 3513–3528); and GLE SC.F.2.3.1 covers reproductive patterns and spores versus seeds in plants (lines 955–966). Florida 2008 separately asks students to relate fungal structure to function (SC.912.L.14.6, lines 3200–3202). Together these support a medium-confidence concept-level link, with substantial grade movement and added fungal/environmental specificity. None explicitly compares fungal reproductive spread under environmental conditions. The fungi–plant connection in SC.3.FG.1.2 has a partial ecological predecessor link; SC.5.FG.1.2 also has a partial GLE link on fungal contributions to soil fertility, plant growth, and resistance to erosion (SC.D.1.3.4, lines 629–642).
  • The four microorganism rows previously unresolved now have partial concept links in the register. The source recheck does not make the draft-specific food-production application, microbial reproduction, or integrated ecosystem/food-system/health analysis equivalent to earlier standards.

Three table candidates also warrant careful scope notes if they are incorporated into the main register: SC.3.CB.1.1 adds applying microscopes to living things beyond the GLE’s generic minute-detail observation; SC.5.HB.1.2 specifies causal effects of changes in one body system beyond general system interaction; and SC.4.FG.1.1 names reciprocal fungi–plant benefit beyond the predecessor’s general mutualism concept. These broader concept matches should not erase the finer-grained divergence.

Additional register matches from source recheck

Six previously unresolved K–5 rows now have medium-confidence partial matches in the register. Florida 1996 SC.A.2.2 states that materials may contain parts too small to see without magnification, supporting the visibility/magnification clause of SC.2.MO.1.1 but not its microorganism/living-things classification. South Carolina 2014 7.L.3A.4 explicitly describes bacteria as both helpful and harmful, supporting the core framing of draft SC.3.MO.1.1; its narrower taxon and grade-7 placement do not support the full microorganism category or grade-3 placement. Florida 1998/99 GLE SC.D.1.3.4 names fungi as contributors to soil fertility, plant growth, and resistance to erosion, supporting part of draft SC.5.FG.1.2; the GLE does not cover all fungal interactions or the full ecosystem-stability task. The same GLE volume names clouds among day/night sky objects in grade 1 (SC.E.2.1.1, lines 416–425), a partial predecessor to kindergarten cloud observation, and states that selected resources used by people for food, water, and shelter are necessary in grade 2 (SC.G.2.1.1, lines 643–656), supporting the draft’s grade-1 human-use-of-living-things concept. Neither source specifies cloud attributes or identifies living things as a distinct resource class; the 2008 SC.4.E.6.3 benchmark adds broad human-resource continuity for the latter. The fungal reproduction crosswalk adds a generic Florida reproduction link, but not the draft’s environmental fungal-dispersal task. These are partial concept matches, not evidence of wording provenance.

Audit limits

No authorship or tracked-change evidence was found or assessed in this residual crosswalk. Concept similarity and grade placement cannot establish text provenance, drafting access, or who introduced a concept. Exact-phrase or document-metadata claims require separate source-file evidence.

Additional 2008 reverse-screen review: elementary carryovers and grade shifts

A source-first check of high-ranked unanchored Florida 2008 records found six specific code-to-benchmark links that the register had previously documented only through GLE or broader source anchors. Florida 2008 SC.1.L.14.2 names the plant parts in grade-1 SC.1.P.1.1; SC.1.L.14.3 directly supports the living/nonliving classification now in kindergarten SC.K.SL.1.1; kindergarten SC.K.E.5.4 supplies a limited predecessor for grade-1 Moon-pattern observations in SC.1.SP.1.2; grade-4 SC.4.P.8.4 matches the magnetic attraction/repulsion concept in SC.4.FM.1.1; and SC.2.P.8.3 plus grade-8 particle-model benchmark SC.8.P.8.4 support distinct levels of the grade-3 matter benchmark SC.3.M.1.1. Each new register anchor is tied to its physical source line and the note distinguishes retained concept from added task, detail, or grade shift. These links reduce the unanchored 2008-source count; they do not establish text transfer.

A further source-level match links Florida 2008 SC.4.E.6.5 to kindergarten SC.K.CB.1.1: both describe tools extending observation of very small things; the draft moves the concept from grade 4 to kindergarten and applies it to small objects and living things, while the 2008 line also includes very large things. The broader large-scale dimension is not stated in that kindergarten benchmark.

The conductors check produced a different result and is recorded as a divergence: 2008 SC.4.P.11.2 and the grade-5 GLE cover thermal conductors, while the 2026 grade-3 benchmark moves that concept earlier. 2008 SC.5.P.11.2 separately requires classification of electrical conductors, and the current clarification explicitly excludes electrical conductivity. No corresponding 1996 passage was located. The register preserves both the carryover and this scope narrowing rather than treating them as equivalent tasks.

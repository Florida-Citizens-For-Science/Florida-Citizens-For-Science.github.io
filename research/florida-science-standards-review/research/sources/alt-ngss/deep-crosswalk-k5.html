Deeper K–5 legacy concept crosswalk

Scope and method

This supplementary pass reviewed the 144 K–5 draft benchmarks that were marked unresolved in the register at the start of the pass. It screened them against the locally preserved Florida 2008 standards, the 1996 Sunshine State Standards grade-band texts, and the 1998–99 Grade Level Expectations for K–2, 3–5, and 6–8. The comparison used the benchmark wording and available explanatory text in those source files. Semantic-search candidates were used to locate passages; similarity scores alone did not establish a match. Every added match was checked against its cited source wording and line anchor. No match is a claim of borrowing or drafting access.

The current K–5 register contains 278 reviewed benchmarks (122 high-confidence concept matches, 155 medium-confidence concept matches, and 1 divergence) and 1 unresolved benchmark, out of 279. Later residual reviews added weather, nutrition/resource, organism, and external-framework comparisons beyond this original pass. The remaining unresolved row concerns the grade-2 food supply-chain task; the current corpus does not establish a sufficiently specific predecessor. Search absence is not coded as divergence or proof of novelty. The grade-3 thermal-conductor benchmark is now marked divergent because it retains the 2008 heat-conduction task at an earlier grade while explicitly excluding the distinct grade-5 electrical-conductivity task.

Benchmark identifiers, per-row notes, source filenames, and physical newline line locators are in 2026-benchmark-register.csv. This pass used the 2008 standards’ benchmark text and cognitive-complexity descriptions, along with the preserved 1996 and 1998–99 texts; it did not assume uncollected clarifications or classroom pacing guidance.

Patterns found

  • Energy and matter move earlier in several sequences. The 2008 grade-5 energy benchmarks identify mechanical and chemical energy and state that energy can cause motion or change (SC.5.P.10.1–.2); corresponding draft concepts occur in grades 1–3. The 2008 closed-circuit benchmark is grade 5 (SC.5.P.11.1), while the draft asks students to build a simple circuit in grade 4. Heat-related physical and chemical change was a grade-5 concept in 2008 (SC.5.P.9.1) and appears in draft grade 2. By contrast, solid/liquid/gas classification remains in grade 2 in both (SC.2.P.8.2 in 2008).
  • Some weather, geology, and astronomy concepts are redistributed across the elementary grades. The older standards address repeating weather patterns, severe-weather preparation, precipitation types, climate zones, and water-cycle modeling mostly in grades 2 and 5. The draft distributes related observations and tasks from kindergarten through grade 4, adding particular tools, clouds, and prediction tasks. The draft also introduces Earth surface-change and fossil topics early; explicit 2008 fossil/evolution treatment is in grade 7 (SC.7.L.15.1). Earth-system interactions are present in the 2008 grade-12 benchmark SC.912.E.7.3, while related draft system-interaction benchmarks appear in grades 4 and 5. These are grade/task comparisons, not judgments that the sequences are pedagogically equivalent.
  • Variation and survival concepts are spread across K–5. Several draft benchmarks on inherited or variable traits, environmental conditions, survival, and reproduction have specific concept counterparts in the 2008 grade-5 standard SC.5.L.15.1 and related heredity, behavior, and life-cycle standards. The older GLE explicitly states that offspring resemble parents (SC.F.2.1.1) and that living structures are adapted to their functions in particular environments (SC.F.1.1.4). The draft distributes these related ideas across multiple grades; the matches do not make every draft task identical to its predecessor.
  • Some K–5 content remains without a specific predecessor match in this corpus. Specific food supply, preparation, and long-term health tasks and comparative fungal reproduction/spread remain unresolved. Other nutrition, microorganism, and fungi benchmarks have partial concept links whose grade shifts and added mechanisms are recorded separately. The unresolved status means only that the reviewed materials did not establish a sufficiently specific predecessor concept.

Limits

The crosswalk records concept continuity and grade placement, not line-by-line textual provenance. Broad standards can support only the concepts they actually name; an adjacent topic or a high retrieval score was not enough. Source versions not preserved in this corpus, unretrieved documents, and instructional materials outside this pass remain outside the corpus. The one currently unresolved K–5 row warrants further source review where provenance or grade-level novelty matters.

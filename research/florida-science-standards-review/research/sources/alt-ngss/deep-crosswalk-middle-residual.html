Grades 6–8 residual crosswalk review

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: current unresolved grades 6–8 rows in 2026-benchmark-register.csv, reviewed against locally preserved 1998/99 GLE, 2008 Florida standards, and other locally held comparison texts.
Purpose: document defensible concept-level predecessor candidates and reconcile them against the current register. This note complements but does not supersede row-level register entries.

At the time of this review, the register contained 39 unresolved middle-grade rows. Eight additional candidates were identified below. A subsequent reconciliation against the current register shows that all eight candidate IDs now have concept-match entries with the cited predecessor locators. A separate review classified SC.8.SP.2.3 as a divergence in explicit cosmological scope. Three later 2008 crosswalk additions reduced the unresolved middle-grade count to 27; a 2008 nature-of-science match for classification systems reduced it to 26. The source-first review added a three-generation stellar-life-cycle match, then a partial cross-version planetary-motion match; the unresolved count was 23 before later fungi-reproduction, electron-organization, and health-risk crosschecks reduced it to 20. Subsequent register updates reduced the live grade 6–8 unresolved count to six, all in the nutrition strand: SC.7.NT.1.6, .1.7, SC.8.NT.1.2, .1.3, .1.4, and .1.6. The earlier deep-pass note reports 42 unresolved, which is stale. The candidate links establish conceptual continuity only; they do not establish wording lift, authorship, access, or provenance. Grade displacement is noted where the located predecessor is at another grade band. A locator is a physical LF line number in the extracted .txt source, not a PDF page or a Python splitlines() offset.

The draft adds the absorbed/released formulation; this is partial continuity.
SC.7.M.1.1 — distinguish physical and chemical properties 1998/99 GLE SC.A.1.3.5, sources/1998-1999/florida-gle-science-68.txt, lines 98–110; related physical-property classification in 2008 SC.8.P.8.4, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 2523–2525. High. GLE explicitly names both physical and chemical properties; 2008 separately supports classifying substances by characteristic physical properties.
SC.7.M.1.2 — use size-independent properties to identify substances 2008 SC.8.P.8.4, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 2523–2525. Medium. The predecessor explicitly says characteristic properties, including density, solubility, and melting/boiling points, are independent of sample amount and can classify/compare substances. Identification of a substance is a close application rather than an identical task.
SC.7.M.2.1 — determine whether a physical or chemical change occurred within a system 1998/99 GLE SC.A.1.3.5, sources/1998-1999/florida-gle-science-68.txt, lines 98–110; 2008 SC.8.P.9.2, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 2564–2566. High. Both sources directly distinguish physical from chemical changes. “Within a system” is an added framing in the draft.
SC.8.M.4.2 — macromolecules are compounds essential to life 2008 SC.912.L.18.1, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 3626–3639. Medium. The predecessor directly identifies the four biological macromolecule categories and asks about their basic molecular structures and functions. It is a high-school benchmark and is more detailed than this middle-grade draft leaf.
SC.8.EN.2.3 — apply wave concepts to explain observed jersey colors 2008 SC.7.P.10.1–.2, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 2248–2252; SC.912.P.10.18, same source, lines 3078–3080. Medium, partial. The predecessors cover the visible-light color spectrum, reflection/refraction/absorption, and the relationships among electromagnetic wavelength, frequency, energy, and phenomena. The grade-8 example adds an investigation of two jersey materials, observer perception, and evidence-based explanation; that application is not stated in the older benchmarks.
SC.7.FM.3.1 — interpret distance-time graphs to describe motion 2008 SC.6.P.12.1, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 2060–2063. High. The predecessor explicitly requires measuring and graphing distance versus time and interpreting the relationship for constant-speed motion. The draft’s task is directly continuous, with a shift from grade 6 to grade 7.
SC.8.EN.1.4 — analyze energy transfer/transformation of electric circuits 2008 SC.5.P.10.4, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 1816–1818, and SC.5.P.11.1, same file, lines 1827–1829. Medium. The first benchmark expressly covers electrical energy transformed into heat, light, sound, or motion; the second requires a closed circuit for electrical flow. Together they support the draft’s core circuit-energy concept, while the draft combines and deepens the task at grade 8.

Medium, partial. The GLE says gravity keeps planets in orbits; the 2008 benchmark names Kepler’s and Newton’s laws in relationships among Earth, Moon, and Sun. The grade-7 draft applies this lineage to distance–orbital-period data, proportional reasoning, and a prediction task; the predecessor wording does not specify that task. This is a grade-shift/content link, not evidence of borrowing.

Additional Florida 2008 system-concept matches

Five system benchmarks previously unresolved now have medium-confidence, partial concept matches to Florida 2008:

Draft benchmark Florida 2008 source and locator Comparison
SC.6.EN.1.1–.3 — identify and distinguish open and closed systems SC.912.P.10.1, sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt, lines 3016–3019 The predecessor explicitly differentiates open, closed, and isolated systems while discussing energy conservation. The 2026 grade-6 sequence defines matter/energy exchange, models system boundaries and inputs/outputs, and asks students to distinguish open from closed cases. This shifts the core classification from high school to grade 6 and adds explicit definitions and models.
SC.7.EN.1.2 — describe system boundaries and identify what is inside/outside SC.912.P.10.1, same source, lines 3016–3019 The predecessor’s system categories presuppose boundaries but do not ask students to identify or label them. The draft makes boundary selection a separate grade-7 modeling task; this is partial continuity with added specificity and an earlier placement.
SC.7.EN.1.4 — explain how matter transfers affect energy flow within a system SC.8.L.18.3, same source, line 2586 The predecessor asks students to model matter and energy as continuously transferred within and between organisms and their physical environment in the carbon cycle. The draft generalizes the coupling and links matter transfer to energy flow; this shifts from grade 8 to grade 7 and broadens beyond the carbon-cycle context.

The cited Florida benchmarks support concept continuity, not the draft’s full definition or instructional sequence, and do not show which sources the 2026 writers consulted.

External comparator resolution

The residual screen found a direct concept match for SC.6.FG.1.1 in South Carolina 2014 grade-6 standard 6.L.5A (physical LF lines 2148–2153): fungi obtain nutrition by external absorption. This is recorded as a high-confidence concept match in the register. Because the South Carolina document postdates Florida’s 2007–08 process, it establishes content parallel only, not provenance or access. After the source checks completed at that time, the register had 13 unresolved grades 6–8 benchmarks.

A later focused check added a medium-confidence, partial concept match for SC.7.FG.1.2: Florida 1996 SC.F.2.4 expectation 1, 2008 SC.912.L.16.14–.17, fungal structure/function SC.912.L.14.6, and the 1998/99 reproduction GLE SC.F.2.3.1 cover general reproduction/spores and fungi. The grade-7 draft’s fungal growth and spore-production responses to temperature/moisture remain more specific than those predecessors. A separate source check also linked SC.7.M.3.4 at medium confidence: the 1998/99 grade-8 GLE states that electrons farther from the nucleus have different energy (SC.A.2.3.2, lines 161–190), while 2008 grade 8 identifies electrons surrounding the nucleus (SC.8.P.8.7, lines 2535–2537) and the 1996 high-school expectation relates electron configuration to reactions and energy (SC.A.1.4, lines 23–27). The 2026 grade-7 benchmark adds explicit energy-level and valence-electron identification plus periodic-pattern analysis. A focused health-row review also linked SC.8.HB.2.2 at medium confidence to 2008 SC.912.L.14.5 (lines 3196–3199), which addresses genetic, environmental, and pathogenic factors affecting individual and public health. The earlier high-school benchmark supports a broad health-risk concept; the grade-8 lifestyle, long-term body-function, and data-analysis task is more specific. A separate review linked grade-8 SC.8.PE.3.2 to 2008 SC.912.P.10.16 and GLE SC.C.2.3.1 for general magnetic-field generation, forces, and electromagnets. The Earth geodynamo application remains specific to the draft. The five 2008 system-concept mappings above reduce the unresolved grades 6–8 set from 18 to 13.

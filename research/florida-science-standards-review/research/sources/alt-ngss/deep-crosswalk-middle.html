Deep predecessor crosswalk: grades 6–8

Research date: October 22, 2026
Scope: unresolved grades 6–8 leaves in 2026-benchmark-register.csv
Purpose: record content continuity and placement only. A concept match does not establish copying, access, authorship, or provenance.

Method

At the start of this pass, 131 of the 227 grades 6–8 draft leaves were unresolved. I reviewed those remaining rows strand by strand against the locally preserved 2008 Florida science standards, the 1996 Sunshine State Standards, and the 1998/99 grade-level expectations (GLEs). The comparison used the full extracted text around standards, benchmark clarifications, and grade-level examples where available. Semantic retrieval and keyword hits were treated as candidate locators only. A match was added only when a cited predecessor passage supported a specific part of the draft benchmark; broad thematic proximity did not qualify. Lack of a match did not become a divergence finding.

The main local sources are the 2008 Florida standards, 1996 standards, grades 6–8, and 1998/99 GLEs, grades 6–8. The 1996 document often states broader strand expectations rather than individual, numbered benchmark tasks. The GLEs add grade-specific detail and examples; these were used to check whether a broad 1996 or 2008 concept had a more precise middle-grade expression. New register entries cite physical line numbers and source paths. All cited source files and line anchors were checked after the final save.

Results

At the time of this initial deep pass, it added 89 concept matches: 39 high-confidence and 50 medium-confidence, leaving 42 of 227 middle-grade leaves unresolved. It added no divergence or non-divergent statuses. Later passes expanded the evidence and revised the current middle-grade totals; the live counts are maintained in the benchmark register and full-draft comparison. Placement and coverage patterns

  • Human body systems: The 2008 grade 6 SC.6.L.14.5 asks students to investigate the major human systems and describe their interactions in maintaining homeostasis. The 1998/99 GLE SC.F.1.3.1 gives grade 6 expectations for how organism systems function and interact (GLE lines 811–822); the 1996 grade-band text likewise identifies major systems, their functions, and structural levels (1996 lines 118–137). The 2026 draft distributes this territory across grades 6–8, with individual digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous, immune, musculoskeletal, excretory, and reproductive benchmarks chiefly in grade 7. This is continuity with a finer-grained sequence, not evidence of instructional pacing or source access.
  • Nutrition: Several grade 6–8 nutrient transport, digestion, and cellular-energy tasks have specific conceptual predecessors, but some detailed 2008 coverage is high-school material (SC.912.L.14.34–.35, SC.912.L.18.8–.9). This suggests some content is moved earlier in the draft. The more specialized draft tasks on hydration and beverages, processed foods, food labels, cost, and nutrition patterns across a lifespan remain unresolved: the older Florida sources reviewed here do not supply a sufficiently specific science benchmark for those formulations. Their unresolved status is not a finding that they are wholly new.
  • Earth and space science: The pass found clear predecessors for Earth’s spheres and atmosphere, interior structure, rock-cycle and surface processes, geologic history, human impacts, Solar System effects on Earth, electromagnetic radiation, and space technology. Several 2026 middle-grade tasks restate or combine older content. In particular, the draft places electromagnetic-spectrum concepts in grade 6 that the 2008 sequence addresses in grades 7–8, and places galaxy classification in grade 6 where the 2008 standards discuss galaxies in grade 8. These are content/grade comparisons, not a judgment about instructional quality.
  • Matter, energy, and waves: Specific overlap includes element grouping, acids and bases, chemical/physical changes, compound formation, energy transformation/conservation, and wave interactions with materials. Some draft formulations add a system boundary, a quantitative relation, or a diagnostic task beyond the older benchmark; these are marked medium confidence where only part of the present task has a predecessor.
  • Living systems: The comparison found continuity for organism hierarchy, photosynthesis and respiration, food-web energy and matter cycling, animal structures/adaptations, human physiology, infectious agents, and the ecological role of fungi and microorganisms. The 1996 standards also address organism behavior, environmental adaptation, classification, and interdependence (1996 lines 143–145 and 170–185). The 1998/99 GLE examples make some concepts more specific: behavior as a response to the environment (SC.F.1.3.7), classification by criteria (SC.G.1.3.3), system interaction (SC.D.1.3.3), and fungi/bacteria adding organic matter to soil (SC.D.1.3.4).

Deliberately unresolved examples

Four candidates were initially left unresolved after the first predecessor-text check: stellar life cycles (SC.6.SP.1.5), patterns in planetary motion (SC.7.SP.1.8), generation and role of Earth’s magnetic field (SC.8.PE.3.2), and classification changing in response to new scientific evidence (SC.6.SL.2.5). Later source-first reviews established continuity for the first two. The 1996/1998/2008 sources trace mass-dependent stellar life cycles and outcomes into the grade-6 draft benchmark; the GLE and 2008 Kepler/Newton benchmark support a partial predecessor for the grade-7 planetary-motion task. Both include grade/task shifts, as detailed in the residual crosswalk. The 1996 standards say scientific knowledge can change with new information (1996 lines 163–165), but that broad nature-of-science concept does not specifically establish a predecessor for changing classification systems. The magnetic-field and classification-system candidates remain unresolved rather than being coded as divergence.

Other unresolved groups include many standalone nutrition benchmarks, several open/closed-system formulations whose predecessors state only general energy conservation, specialized fungal/microbial mechanisms, and the six applied-synthesis (ASL) benchmarks. A related standard or broad scientific topic was not enough to assign a predecessor.

Limits and use

This pass improves benchmark-level coverage but does not complete source-by-source adjudication of the full 2026 corpus. The remaining 42 middle-grade rows require further review or stronger predecessor evidence. Grade comparisons identify the grade labels in the source standards; they do not establish the order of instruction within a school year. The cited 2008, 1996, and GLE files are the local research corpus versions listed in the repository inventory. Other source editions or intermediary documents may change the comparison. No claim about textual borrowing or unauthorized access follows from these concept mappings.

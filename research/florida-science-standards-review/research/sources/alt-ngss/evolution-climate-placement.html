2026 draft register and evolution/climate placement crosswalk

Research cutoff: 2026-10-22. This appendix compares grade placement and coverage, not wording provenance.

Register scope and method

2026-benchmark-register.csv contains all 1,501 unique leaf benchmark IDs parsed from the three 2026 draft text extracts: 277 K–5, 227 grades 6–8, and 997 grades 9–12. Duplicate-ID check found none. Each row records the benchmark ID, its grade, the first benchmark statement (excluding examples and clarifications), the page index in the matching draft PDF/text extraction, a crosswalk status, the cited predecessor anchors where assessed, and a note. All rows have been candidate-screened. The current manual review gives 35 rows diverges, 3 feature-specific non-divergent, 348 high-confidence and 969 medium-confidence concept matches, and 146 unresolved vs research corpus. Concept-match rows show continuity of an idea but do not automatically classify every dimension as non-divergent. Unresolved rows are not thereby “new” or “absent”; they need additional source material or clarification-level comparison for a reliable verdict.

The crosswalk below uses three statuses:

  • Non-divergent: the predecessor corpus clearly contains the same core instructional concept; no material difference in the feature described is established here.
  • Diverges: a grade placement, sequence, scope, or explicitness difference is visible in the cited benchmark text.
  • Unresolved vs research corpus: the available predecessor files or mapping are insufficient for a reliable conclusion.

These labels describe the specific comparison in each row, not the entire standard or its presumed drafting source. “Continuity” is not proof of textual borrowing.

Evolution and population change

Version Placement and sequence Normalized comparison Status
1996 Sunshine State Standards Grades 6–8: survival characteristics and fossil-record evidence of change (SC.F.2.3.3–.4); adaptations enhancing reproductive success (SC.G.1.3.2). Grades 9–12: mechanisms of change including mutation and natural selection, adaptation and diversity (SC.F.2.4.3); genetic variation and selection (SC.G.1.4.3). Fossil evidence and adaptation are in middle grades; mechanisms of evolutionary change are explicit in high school. Non-divergent with the broad two-stage progression in the 2026 draft.
1998/99 Grade Level Expectations Grade 6: adaptations aid survival. Grade 8: adaptations to environment and fossil-record change (SC.F.2.3.3–.4, GLE printed p.25). The 6th/7th GLE entries for fossil evidence explicitly defer content to grade 8. The GLE narrows placement inside the 1996 6–8 band. It does not specify a middle-school natural-selection mechanism in the reviewed 6–8 text. Diverges from 2008’s grade 7 natural-selection emphasis; aligns more closely with 2026’s grade 8 location for the explicit mechanism.
2008 NGSSS Grade 7: fossil evidence consistent with evolution (SC.7.L.15.1), genetic variation/environmental factors and natural selection (.15.2), and failure to adapt/extinction (.15.3), printed pp.48–49. High school: Big Idea 15 SC.912.L.15.1–.15, including evidence, speciation, Hardy–Weinberg, natural-selection conditions (.15.13) and other mechanisms (.15.14). Evolution mechanisms are named in grade 7 and revisited in a detailed high-school sequence. Diverges from 2026 by one grade for the explicit middle-school mechanism.
2026 review draft Grade 8: SC.8.SL.2.1–.2.4 covers selection conditions, inherited variation and survival/reproduction, population change over time, and natural vs artificial selection (draft pp.136–140); SC.8.SL.3.1–.3.2 addresses evidence and fossil-record change (pp.141–142). High school: SC.912.SL.6.1–.6.6 distributes variation, survival, reproductive success, population response, and explanations across a sequence (draft pp.199–203). Core selection concepts persist, with a shift from grade 7 in 2008 to grade 8 in 2026. The current draft gives grade 8 an explicit mechanism sequence and retains related population-change work in high school. “Natural selection” is explicit in the grade-8 strand; in the located high-school sequence it appears in a historical-development clarification (SC.912.SL.6.5 clarification 7), rather than as the named umbrella benchmark used in 2008. Diverges in grade placement and architecture; non-divergent in core concepts.

Normalized takeaway: Compared with 1996, the 2026 draft keeps evolutionary mechanisms in both the middle and high-school span, but specifies them more directly in grade 8. Compared with the 2008 grade 7 benchmark cluster, the named middle-school natural-selection sequence moves one grade later. The draft does not eliminate high-school population-change content: it reorganizes it under SL.6 and makes data analysis, survival, reproduction, and trait distributions prominent. The available documents establish placement/content continuity, not which historical source a drafter consulted.

Climate and climate-system coverage

Version Placement and sequence Normalized comparison Status
1996 Sunshine State Standards Grades 9–12: changes in Earth’s climate, geology, and life forms can be traced and compared; Earth systems and organisms result from continuous change (SC.D.1.4.3–.4, printed p.2). Fossil-fuel combustion releases stored energy as heat and carbon dioxide (SC.G.2.4.1, printed p.3). Climate is one of several long-term Earth changes; CO₂ release is stated, but these cited benchmarks do not explicitly identify fossil-fuel CO₂ as a cause of contemporary global climate change. Non-divergent for climate-system change and carbon-emission concepts; attribution wording is not equivalent.
1998/99 GLEs Reviewed K and 6–8 GLE texts include climate as a habitat/geographic condition but no located “climate change,” “global warming,” or “greenhouse” benchmark language. Climate-change absence is limited to these surviving GLE files. These files do not document a grade-by-grade climate-change sequence. Unresolved for a complete 1998/99 climate crosswalk; the GLE corpus is partial and is not a replacement standards text.
2008 NGSSS Grade 6: water cycle/weather/climate, jet stream/ocean-current effects, and weather–climate distinction (SC.6.E.7.2–.6, printed p.43). High school: conditions shaping regional climate (SC.912.E.7.4), ocean influence on climate change through heat/carbon/water storage and movement (.7.6), and internal Earth-system plus external astronomical conditions contributing to global climate change (.7.7, printed pp.59–60). The 2008 text makes “climate change” and global-climate-change contributing conditions explicit in high school. Diverges from the 2026 climate strand’s primary framing as climate regulation and system interaction; note overlapping ocean/energy content.
2026 review draft Grade 6: solar energy/greenhouse role (SC.6.PE.1.2) and natural vs anthropogenic Earth-system changes (.1.3; draft p.73). Grade 8: weather/climate, climate-pattern factors and Earth-system interactions over time (SC.8.PE.1.1–.1.5, .2.1; pp.86–91); clarification explicitly includes natural processes and anthropogenic influences. High school: SC.912.PE.13.1–.13.6 covers radiation balance, greenhouse processes, atmospheric/ocean circulation, feedbacks, and integrated climate regulation (pp.143–146); SC.912.PE.16 addresses anthropogenic Earth-system effects (from p.155). Draft expands the mechanistic account of how climate is regulated and introduces natural/anthropogenic system distinctions by middle school. The high-school climate-regulation cluster says greenhouse-concentration changes affect climate but does not explicitly name human-caused global climate change in that cluster. Climate-related hazards and impacts are distributed elsewhere. Diverges in framing and sequence; unresolved whether coverage is more or less complete overall than 2008 without a full benchmark-by-benchmark crosswalk.

Normalized takeaway: The 2008 standards explicitly ask high-school students to identify and relate internal and astronomical conditions contributing to global climate change (SC.912.E.7.7). The 2026 draft emphasizes climate-system regulation—radiation budget, greenhouse gases, circulation and feedbacks—and places a natural/anthropogenic distinction in grade 8. It does not repeat the 2008 benchmark’s specific formulation of global climate-change contributors in the cited climate cluster. At the same time, it does include anthropogenic Earth-system effects in SC.912.PE.16 and climate-pattern change in grade 8. This is a redistribution and reframing finding, not a conclusion that climate change has been removed.

Corpus limits and source anchors

  • 1996: four surviving Florida standards PDFs cover PreK–2, 3–5, 6–8, and 9–12. The files are preserved scans/exports, not the complete original curriculum-framework publication. The 6–8 item is independently matched to an Internet Archive capture. Repository landing page: USF/FCIT Sunshine State Standards; archived 6–8 source: Internet Archive, science6.pdf.
  • 1998/99: retained files are Grade Level Expectations, not evidence of a separately adopted 1998 replacement standards set. The 6–8 GLE is cited by its printed page and benchmark ID; the unavailable high-school GLE prevents a complete vertical comparison. The contemporaneous 1998 review says the added grade expectations retained the 1996 benchmark statements; see ERIC ED439133.
  • 2008: local PDF is a mirror; FDOE confirms the adopted standards history at the official Florida science standards page. The benchmark citations above use the PDF’s printed page numbers, which differ by one from pdftotext page indexing in some locations.
  • 2026: draft is from FDOE’s standards review page. Page numbers above are PDF page indices matching the text extracts. The PDFs are drafts under review, not adopted standards.
  • There is no recovered full 2026 source DOCX/version history here. This appendix cannot establish who moved or revised the benchmarks, when those changes occurred, or which earlier text was used.

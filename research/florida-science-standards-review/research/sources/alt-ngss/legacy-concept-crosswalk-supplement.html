Legacy concept crosswalk supplement: audited leaf examples

Research date: October 22, 2026
Purpose: Follow-up concept-level comparison of unresolved rows in 2026-benchmark-register.csv against the locally preserved 2008 Florida NGSSS, 1996 Sunshine State Standards, and 1998/99 Grade Level Expectations (GLEs). This records predecessor concept presence and grade placement only. It is not evidence of drafting access, borrowing, or direct textual provenance.

Proportion resolved in this pass

The register began with 1,501 unique 2026 leaf benchmarks: 33 marked diverges or non-divergent and 1,468 unresolved. A first manual pass added nine concept matches (8 high confidence; 1 medium). A second pass over the 141 high-score leads added 65 individually verified concept matches (50 high confidence; 15 medium). A third conservative pass over the 67 still-unresolved high-score leads added 39 more (16 high confidence; 23 medium). A fourth pass reviewed 250 additional unresolved rows below the prior high-score cutoff, stratified across grade bands and strands, and added 93 concept matches (49 high confidence; 44 medium). A fifth pass screened 500 further unresolved rows, allocated across bands and proportionally across strands within each band, and added 90 individually verified concept matches (59 high confidence; 31 medium). Two other examples reviewed in the first pass (SC.8.SL.3.2, already non-divergent, and SC.912.SL.6.5, already diverges) had prior explicit statuses and were not recounted as additions. Before the sixth pass, the register had 329/1,501 rows with explicit comparison status. At that historical checkpoint after the sixth and seventh passes, 437/1,501 (29.1%) had explicit status: 215 high-confidence and 189 medium-confidence concept matches, 23 divergences, and 10 non-divergent comparisons; 1,064 remained unresolved. The new passes added 108 concept matches and did not assign divergence or non-divergent labels. These are bounded, manually reviewed results, not an estimate of how many 2026 topics have historical antecedents. The fifth pass resolved 90 (18.0%) of its 500 sampled rows; it assigned no divergence or non-divergent labels.

A semantic retrieval screen produced candidate results for every leaf. At the conservative screening thresholds (best source-family score at least .33 for 2008 and GLE, or .30 for 1996), it raised 141 unique unresolved rows for follow-up. Those 141 were leads, not resolutions; 28 remain unresolved after the first three passes. The fourth pass selected 250 other unresolved rows below that cutoff, balanced by grade band (84 K–5, 83 grades 6–8, and 83 grades 9–12) and proportionally distributed among their strands. It mapped 93 of those 250; 157 remain unresolved. Ranking errors included unrelated matches (e.g., animal-reproduction queries returning the 2008 brain-parts benchmark; Solar-System-location queries returning a broad cosmology paragraph). The score threshold is not a confidence label and must not be treated as proof.

Candidate leads left unresolved

  • SC.912.PE.13.2 (greenhouse processes and climate patterns) retrieves 2008 SC.912.E.7.6–.7 as the nearest conceptual passage. Those 2008 benchmarks discuss ocean effects and conditions contributing to climate change, but do not provide a clear benchmark-level predecessor for the specific greenhouse-process mechanism in the 2026 leaf. Keep unresolved at this granularity.
  • Numerous 2026 specialized life-science benchmarks retrieve broad 2008 umbrellas such as SC.912.L.14 (organization/development), .15 (evolution), and .17 (interdependence). Those family-level matches establish a likely conceptual neighborhood, not that each specific 2026 leaf has a counterpart. Retain row-level unresolved status absent a more specific predecessor.
  • GLE files contain K–2, 3–5, and 6–8 material only in the surviving corpus; no high-school GLE crosswalk should be inferred from their absence.

Method and limitations

  1. Confirmed 1,501 register rows and counted original status values (23 diverges, 10 non-divergent, 1,468 unresolved). The first pass updated nine individually reviewed unresolved rows. The second pass checked the strongest 141 retrieval leads and added 65 concept matches. A third pass checked the 67 threshold leads still unresolved and added 39. A fourth pass reviewed 250 lower-score unresolved rows, balanced by grade band and distributed across strands, and added 93. A fifth pass reviewed the next 500 unresolved rows and added 90. A sixth pass reviewed 500 further unresolved rows from the residual pool and added 54; a seventh screen covered the final 81 untouched high-school rows and added 54. Across these screened sets, rows without individually supported matches remain unresolved.
  2. For each draft benchmark, queried the local index against chunks under sources/2008/, sources/1996/, and sources/1998-1999/. The index metadata documents TF-IDF plus truncated-SVD LSI, scored 0.55 lexical cosine plus 0.45 latent cosine. The checked-in CLI had been changed to look for a nested .semantic-search/lsi/ directory that this top-level index does not have; this pass read the existing top-level vectors and applied the same scoring and source-path restrictions. Full top-two retrievals are retained in legacy-crosswalk-candidates.csv.
  3. Used ranked output only to surface likely matches. For each added row, checked the source standard or GLE text and recorded its ID and physical text-line locator, then classified concept and grade fit manually. We did not raise status on semantic score alone. Several candidates were rejected because the retrieved chunk was generic or unrelated (notably repeated grade-9–12 hits on a brain-anatomy benchmark for animal reproduction). The fourth through seventh passes assigned no new divergence or non-divergent labels because absence from candidate passages alone does not establish divergence. Later-pass self-audits removed weak matches, including solar-radiation text for a chemical-reaction energy benchmark, general cell theory for a prokaryotic-structure benchmark, and broad Earth-history hits for unrelated Earth/space leaves.

This remains a targeted, non-random manual pass, not an exhaustive adjudication of all 1,468 originally unresolved rows. The retrieval index uses 1,500-character chunks, so neighboring standards can contaminate candidate text; it also has no explicit grade-aware scoring. The 1996 files are incomplete surviving scans; GLE files are partial, omit high-school GLEs, and often distribute grade expectations under a broader benchmark. Concepts are repeated across versions with different granularity, so “same topic” is insufficient. The 404 newly mapped concept rows and all 1,172 originally unresolved rows now individually screened (including the high-score leads, stratified samples, and final residual review) should not be extrapolated as a corpus-wide prevalence estimate. Most new mappings trace continuity in older Florida frameworks; they do not resolve who drafted the 2026 text or whether any source was consulted.

The register now includes 404 manually reviewed concept-level crosswalk updates across seven passes. Local PDF/text sources and hashes are recorded in ../../SHA256SUMS.txt and the register appendix evolution-climate-placement.md.

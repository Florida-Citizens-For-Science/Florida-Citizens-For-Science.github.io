Source-first task-equivalence review: unanchored 1996 Florida benchmarks

Scope and method

This appendix reviews the 22 unique 1996 Florida source IDs in sources/alt-ngss/legacy-reverse-screen.csv with direct_anchor_in_register=no. I read the preserved grade-band extract at each source locator and checked the corresponding PDF page where the extract or row text needed context. The numbered expectations belong to one source benchmark ID; they are not counted as separate rows. I then searched the 2026 K–5, 6–8, and 9–12 extracts for equivalent tasks and concepts, checking adjacent benchmark wording, examples, clarifications, and student tasks where those affect the judgment. CSV semantic-neighbor leads were used only as locators.

The source locators identify the .txt extract line and the printed page in the preserved PDF. Draft locators identify the benchmark line in the current .txt extract; relevant text continues in the same benchmark section unless otherwise stated. PDF page breaks can interrupt a benchmark section, so line anchors are more precise for the draft wording.

R means the central source tasks/concepts are retained or relocated across the current draft. P means some source material appears, while a meaningful task or concept is narrowed, implicit, or absent from the reviewed text. O means a possible corpus-bounded omission of a source-specific task/concept; it does not prove classroom absence or establish provenance. U means the comparison remains unresolved. These are task-equivalence judgments, not claims about drafting history.

Coverage check

The row review contains 22 distinct source IDs, each once. These are the complete 1996 Florida rows matching direct_anchor_in_register=no in the current reverse-screen CSV. Repeated numbered expectations within a code were treated as components of that one source ID. The R/P/O/U totals above therefore count source benchmarks, not numbered expectations.

The O classification is a corpus-bounded candidate only. It records that the interpersonal “quality of life of those around them” task was not located in the preserved current science extracts; it is not proof of absence from classrooms, instruction beyond this corpus, other documents, or evidence of provenance.

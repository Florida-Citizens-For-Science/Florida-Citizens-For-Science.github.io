Source-first review of unanchored 2008 middle-school rows

Result

Reviewed 37 unanchored 2008 Florida standards rows for grades 6–8 against the current 2026 draft extracts and appendices. The source locator in each row is the 2008 extracted-text line and the matching PDF page. Classifications are concept-continuity judgments: R retained/relocated, P partial, O possible omission, U unresolved. O means the specifically named concept was not located after checking the available K–12 draft extracts and relevant appendices; it is a research lead, not proof of omission. No row is classified U.

The strongest grade shifts are the matter and particle expectations in SC.8.P.8.1, now substantially represented in grade 6 (SC.6.M.1 and SC.6.M.3), and heat flow in SC.7.P.11.4, whose warmer-to-cooler direction appears in grade 3 (SC.3.EN.1.5). Science-practice expectations are mostly distributed through the 2026 foreword, STARS framework and discipline-specific laboratory/modeling appendices rather than retained as standalone N benchmarks. Several details remain only partial or possible omissions, including hypothesis value, replication by others versus repeated trials, science/pseudoscience distinctions, public and political influences on science, solar features, and temperature as a cause of chemical change.

Method and corpus

For every source item, I read the full 2008 passage and compared it with candidate and neighboring 2026 grade 6–8 rows, then checked applicable K–5 and 9–12 rows and the 2026 common material. The source line numbers below were reconciled against sources/2008/florida-2008-science-standards.txt; PDF pages are 1-based and match source_pdf_page_index in sources/alt-ngss/2008-reverse-screen.csv. Draft line locators refer to the extracted files, not PDF page numbers.

Checked draft material: sources/2026/fl-k5.txt, sources/2026/fl-68.txt, sources/2026/fl-912.txt, and sources/2026/fl-tables.txt, including benchmark clarifications, examples, student tasks, STARS, laboratory practices, modeling skills and timelines. Common references used repeatedly include the foreword’s inquiry/evidence/modeling framing (sources/2026/fl-foreword.txt:78-84,119-146), 6–8 energy/matter/FM modeling skills (fl-tables.txt:1705-1743) and laboratory practices (1749-1798), Planet Earth/space lab practices (2079-2119) and timeline (2125-2138), and life-science practices (2764-2801). The appendix practices are integrated supports; their presence does not establish that every older standalone practice concept is explicitly taught.

Follow-up leads

The five O rows merit targeted follow-up against any newly located 2026 ancillary materials or implementation guidance: SC.6.N.1.4 (same-investigation group comparison and negotiation), SC.8.N.1.4 (hypotheses that motivate further investigation), SC.8.N.2.1 (science/pseudoscience distinction), SC.8.N.4.2 (reciprocal social/political/economic effects on science), and SC.8.P.9.3 (temperature’s influence on chemical change). Partial rows most likely to benefit from a closer curriculum/materials check include SC.7.N.1.2 (replication vs repetition), SC.8.N.1.3 (support vs proof), SC.8.N.3.2 (theories rarely discarded), SC.8.E.5.6 (sunspots/prominences), and SC.8.E.5.8 (geocentric/heliocentric comparison).

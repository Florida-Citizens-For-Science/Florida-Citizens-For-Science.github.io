Source-first task-equivalence audit: anchored 1998/99 GLE grades 6–8

Scope and result

This appendix reviews all 43 rows in sources/alt-ngss/legacy-reverse-screen.csv where source_family=1998/99 GLE, source_grade_band=68, and direct_anchor_in_register=yes. It combines the 32 rows reviewed in the prior pass with 11 other directly anchored rows that were not yet included in this appendix. Every filtered source ID appears exactly once below. A register anchor or similarity candidate is only a locator; the finding compares the full GLE benchmark and grade-level expectations with the relevant 2026 benchmark text and its examples, tasks, clarifications, and nearby content.

Class Count
R — retained/relocated 23
P — partial 20
O — possible omission of a source-specific task/concept 0
U — unresolved 0
Total 32

O is a corpus-bounded candidate only: it would mean the source-specific task/concept was not located in the reviewed corpus, not that it is absent from classroom instruction or that a particular drafting history caused its absence. P marks meaningful continuity with a material detail or task demand narrowed, moved, or not explicitly stated. R includes grade shifts and concepts distributed across several benchmarks. U is for items the corpus does not allow us to assess.

Method and locator notes

I read the complete 1998/99 benchmark plus its Sixth, Seventh, and Eighth Grade Level Expectations from sources/1998-1999/florida-gle-science-68.txt and checked the corresponding pages in sources/1998-1999/florida-gle-science-68.pdf. For each row, I compared all listed direct-anchor benchmark(s) with neighboring content in sources/2026/fl-k5.txt, fl-68.txt, and fl-912.txt, including benchmark examples, student tasks, and clarifications where those explain the expected action or scope. A code match alone did not count as retention.

The CSV supplies source page and line locators for these 43 rows; I checked them against the extract and PDF. In the table, source page numbers are the printed PDF page numbers, and extract lines use ordinary newline-delimited line numbering. Draft references use the extract file’s newline-delimited line numbers. Several 2026 IDs are printed with inconsistent spacing (for example, SC.8. FM...); the locators below preserve the code’s intended normalized spelling and point to the line as printed. A few standards occupy a code-only line followed by task text, so the locator identifies the benchmark start; the comparison included its following task and clarification block.

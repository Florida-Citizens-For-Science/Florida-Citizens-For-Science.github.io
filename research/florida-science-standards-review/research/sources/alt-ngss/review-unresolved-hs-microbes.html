Review of unresolved 2026 high-school fungi and microorganism benchmarks

Review date: October 22, 2026
Scope: All 32 entries currently marked unresolved vs research corpus in the 2026 benchmark register whose IDs begin SC.912.FG. or SC.912.MO. (16 in each strand). Draft wording and benchmark clarifications/examples were checked in sources/2026/fl-912.txt; comparison was made against all locally held Florida 2008 and 1996 text, the available 1998–99 GLE volumes, and the available NGSS/NRC, Massachusetts 2006, and South Carolina 2014 material. The full source texts were searched by concept terms and then relevant passages were read in context. The tables record the closest useful locators; none located means no sufficiently relevant passage in the checked corpus, not absence from every resource or classroom.

Interpretation

Recommendations concern content correspondence and the next register disposition, not provenance. Medium concept match means one or more components are stated in an earlier Florida benchmark or an external standard, while the 2026 benchmark adds a more specific task, organism, mechanism, or context. Divergence means the central concept/task was not located in the examined corpus and appears as additional stated scope. Remain unresolved is used when the closest sources are too broad to settle even a useful concept match. No row was judged non-divergent: none of the located sources states the full benchmark at the same scope. A match—even a close content match—does not establish access, borrowing, authorship, or intent.

Florida’s available GLE text ends at grades 6–8; there is no high-school 1998–99 GLE file in this corpus. Where a same-grade predecessor is not available, this review does not infer that a high-school concept was never taught. The 2026 PDF assigns these benchmarks to the grade 9–12 band but supplies no school-year calendar or semester placement. The repeated page order in fl-912.txt cannot establish instructional timing.

Cross-cutting findings and limits

Strongest external content comparators

  • South Carolina 2014 fungi: 6.L.5A explicitly covers fungal nutrition by external absorption, fruiting structures, and responses to environmental stimuli (south-carolina-2014.txt:2144–2161). It is the closest locally held external subject-matter comparator for FG.6.1–.2, FG.7.1, FG.7.4, and parts of FG.11.1–.2. It does not supply the draft’s specific hyphal mechanisms, reproductive-structure list, or environmental-condition/reproduction linkage.
  • Massachusetts 2006 and South Carolina 2014 cell biology: Massachusetts 2.1 enumerates nucleolus, cytoplasm, ER, Golgi, ribosome and other cell parts and says to relate them to functions (massachusetts-2006.txt:2532–2534). South Carolina H.B.2B.1 asks for models of cell structures interacting to produce, modify, and transport proteins (south-carolina-2014.txt:3040–3048); H.B.2B.2 compares fungal cells with other cell types (:3048–3051). These are close topic-level comparators for several FG rows, but not fungal-specific mechanism matches.
  • South Carolina viruses/cells: H.B.2B.3 directly contrasts virus and cell structure and explains host dependence (south-carolina-2014.txt:3053–3054); Massachusetts 2.8 compares genetic material and reproduction (massachusetts-2006.txt:2550). These are the clearest external comparators for MO.5.1. They do not match the ecosystem claim in MO.5.5.
  • South Carolina microbial energy: H.B.3A.4–.5 covers aerobic/anaerobic respiration and fermentation (south-carolina-2014.txt:3110–3127). It is a useful content comparator for MO.3.3, .3.5, and .9.1, while lacking the target’s microbial pathway flexibility/trade-off analysis and specific food uses.
  • Massachusetts biotechnology definition: “Biotechnology” includes developing microorganisms for specific uses (massachusetts-2006.txt:6779–6783). This is broad comparator language for MO.9.1–.5, not an application-specific match. The actual draft’s named examples need separate provenance work if they are of interest.

Florida baseline and omissions boundary

Florida 2008 has broad fungal structure/function (SC.912.L.14.7), general organelle comparisons (SC.6.L.14.4), prokaryote/eukaryote organization (SC.912.L.14.3), virus/pathogen coverage (SC.6.L.14.6, SC.912.L.14.5), viruses/bacteria transferring genetic material (SC.912.L.16.7), cell reproduction and spores (SC.912.L.16.14–.17), ecosystem succession, population interactions, producers/decomposers, and cycles (SC.912.L.17.4–.10). Florida 1996 supplies broad cell/genetic regulation, reproduction, adaptation, and ecosystem concepts (florida-1996-science-912.txt:134–180). Available GLEs add grade 6–8 cell-system language (florida-gle-science-68.txt:890–906), virus dependence/disease (:1037–1047), fungal/bacterial effects on soil (:630–640), and spore/seed comparison (:966–968). These are meaningful partial precedents but do not resolve the detailed fungal and microbial mechanisms enumerated in the 2026 rows.

Accordingly, five rows are recommended as divergence from the compared corpus at their central specified scope (FG.4.4, MO.4.1, MO.5.5, MO.7.3, and MO.8.5); the other rows have at least a broad or partial content comparator and are recommended as medium concept matches or remain unresolved. This is a research-corpus coverage judgment. It does not prove those concepts were not included in uncollected Florida curriculum materials.

Provenance and text-extraction cautions

No exact distinctive phrase sequence linking these 2026 rows to a prior or external source was established in this review. Some wording is standard disciplinary vocabulary; topic overlap is not evidence that a source was accessed. South Carolina 2014 and Massachusetts 2006 are useful leads for exact-version phrase comparison and records review, not evidence that the 2026 working group used them. The high-school GLE gap remains material. Authorship/change-history metadata in original source files is outside this text-only comparison and must be inspected separately before drawing claims about process access.

The draft extraction has several handling cautions visible in fl-912.txt: repeated Proposed DRAFT Standards/review-only page furniture is interleaved with benchmark text; ligatures are preserved as Unicode characters (ﬁ, ﬂ); some benchmark statements wrap across lines; and the PDF page number in the register is not the physical line number in the text extract. Search and phrase comparison should normalize ligatures and whitespace while preserving a raw-text copy for verification against the PDF. No target benchmark ID appeared duplicated or missing during this review, but this is not a complete PDF parser audit.

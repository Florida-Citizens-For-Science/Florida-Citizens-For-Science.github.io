Source comparison · 2024–2026

The Franklin Standards and Florida’s 2026 science draft

Larry Plank’s October 3 LinkedIn article argues that Florida’s proposed standards resemble the Franklin Standards. We obtained the NAS model standards, indexed the complete document in the private corpus, and compared its content and wording with the three Florida grade-band drafts. The source is a useful comparator. The evidence does not establish that Florida copied, adopted, or was directed by the Franklin project.

Finding: The documents differ in how explicitly they frame evolution, climate change, Earth’s age, and the universe’s age. A normalized wording screen found common, generic science formulations but no distinctive extended match. No Florida drafting record, source citation, or other documentary link to Franklin was found.

These are content comparisons, not claims that Florida is required to adopt another organization’s model. They also should be read alongside the Florida 2008 crosswalk: that adopted framework explicitly covered evolution, climate change, methods for measuring Earth’s age, and the Big Bang. The universe-age number in Franklin is an added comparator; the 2008 Florida standard did not itself set a numerical age requirement. Wording and provenance check

We compared normalized eight-word sequences across the extracted 133-page Franklin PDF and the three Florida grade-band text files. The screen found 27 unique shared sequences. Review of these matches found routine formulations, such as conservation of energy and mass, the order of the electromagnetic spectrum, and Newton’s third law—not distinctive, extended passages. This is a bounded text check, not proof that no influence occurred. Common scientific content and Franklin’s own acknowledged sources (Massachusetts 2006 and New York 1996 curricula) are plausible shared pathways.

The available records do not document a Florida adoption decision, commission, citation, or drafting link to the Franklin Standards. The public Florida PDFs lack source bibliographies and version history. Accordingly, “Florida follows the Franklin playbook” is Larry Plank’s interpretation in the LinkedIn article, not a conclusion established by this review. The comparison is evidence about the texts’ content, not about intent or authorship.

Source and preservation

The National Association of Scholars and Freedom in Education publish The Franklin Standards: Model K–12 State Science Standards (2024). The current NAS report page links a 9.25 PDF; its PDF metadata records creation in September 2025. The private project corpus retains the downloaded 133-page file and a page-extracted text copy with SHA-256 hashes and page locators. The full document is not redistributed on this site because the NAS source is copyrighted and no general open license was identified; see the publisher’s terms and conditions.

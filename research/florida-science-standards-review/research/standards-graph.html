Explore the standards and source graph

Search benchmark text, move between related items, and open the source or review record. Similarity and a graph connection indicate a documented comparison; they do not establish copying, authorship, or access.

What is included: benchmark items from the 1996 Florida standards, 1998/99 grade-level elaborations, 2008 adopted standards, the 2026 proposed draft, NGSS, Indiana 2000, and the 2007 Florida Citizens for Science proposal, plus 15 document-level records for ancillary process and framework sources. Related concepts and comparison assertions are shown where recorded. Items are served from the reviewed public graph; source files remain at their host unless redistribution has been checked.

Graph coverage has expanded; review links are not all equally supported. The graph now includes all 498 Florida 2008 benchmark IDs, all 208 NGSS Performance Expectations, 10 complete grade-level blocks from the 2007 Florida Citizens for Science proposal, and all 412 Indiana K–8 statements. It also contains 3,354 machine-ranked similarity leads. Those are unreviewed discovery aids, not evidence of borrowing, continuity, or omission. A set of 157 derived legacy crosswalk items cite multiple source files and have no single source-document assignment. See the source-coverage notes before drawing conclusions from the map.

Curriculum materials: a focused first pass has verified 19 OpenSciEd resource matches. Other draft benchmarks remain “Not inventoried”; the search has not covered all publishers or all 1,512 benchmarks. A missing entry is not evidence that no material exists. Source standards themselves are not counted as curriculum materials.

Select a node in either the map or search results to center the network on that item. Choose a connection depth to expand or narrow its recorded neighborhood. “Connection” follows the graph’s undirected recorded assertions; source-document bookkeeping links are excluded. Use the relationship details for direction, evidence notes, and confidence values.

Design basis: graph-exploration research recommends meaningful grouping, filtering, and linked overview/detail views to manage visual complexity; recent network-design work frames exploration as map-like wayfinding with search, filters, and selectable neighborhoods. The source map, benchmark view, filters, edge selection, and item detail apply those ideas. Interactive SVG keyboard semantics follow the W3C SVG Accessibility API Mappings. See Shneiderman & Dunne on interactive network exploration and Cerioli et al. on network wayfinding.

Reading the connections

Connections retain the graph’s relationship label, evidence note, confidence where recorded, and method. “Candidate predecessor” and “possible omission” are review judgments, not established text transmission. Dashed purple links in the focused network are machine-ranked similarity leads; the source map excludes them from counts so they cannot appear as reviewed source relationships.

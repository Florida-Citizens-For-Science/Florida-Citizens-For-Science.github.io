Evolution, ancestry, climate, and Earth history

  • Middle school SC.8.SL.3.1 asks students to identify and evaluate evidence supporting the scientific theory of evolution.
  • High school SC.912.SL.6.5 asks how multiple lines of evidence support evolution. SC.912.SL.6.7 includes hominid examples and a clarification identifying common ancestry and descent with modification as central concepts.
  • The exact phrase “climate change” appears three times in the DOCX: once in a high-school mammal-impact clarification under SC.912.A.12.8 and twice in support tables concerning historical climate reconstructions. It appears in no numbered benchmark statement. “Global warming” does not appear. Separate anthropogenic Earth-system content remains present.
  • Approximate ages appear in clarifications: Earth (~4 billion years) under SC.7.PE.3.2; universe (~13.8 billion years) under SC.6.SP.1.2. Neither is a separate benchmark statement.

Source tracing and viewpoint assessment

The register searches the local comparison corpus for shared exact five-word phrases in each removed or added passage. It lists matched reference files and phrase counts. These are candidates for source coverage, not evidence that a drafter used a source. When no text overlap was found, the report records that result and does not infer that a concept lacks references. A full semantic task-by-task crosswalk still requires reading the named source passage. Likewise, viewpoint notes flag topical relevance but do not infer motive from edits alone.

Scope of the appendix

The two registers compare coded parent descriptions and coded benchmark records, including benchmark examples, clarifications, and tasks retained in those records. To limit redistribution of the source text, public registers contain locators, word counts, reference-overlap candidates, and viewpoint notes rather than complete before/after passages. They do not constitute a cell-by-cell redline of the 149 tables in the November DOCX against the separately published October support-table PDF and online forms. Those supplemental materials remain distinct from the coded standards and need a separate comparison before making claims about every table change.

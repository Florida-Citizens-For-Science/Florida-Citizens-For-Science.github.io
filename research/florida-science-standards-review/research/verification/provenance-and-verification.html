Provenance and verification report

This report records what we checked across the standards-review site, how we checked it, what changed when claims failed verification, and what remains open. It is a dated audit record, not a guarantee that every interpretation is final.

Audit cutoff: October 29, 2026. The review covers the 64 HTML pages in this standards-review project and the linked analysis, bibliography, data, and graph files. Source files are retained in the private research corpus; this site publishes public source links, non-sensitive methods, hashes, and verification registers rather than redistributing copyrighted documents.

Plan applied

  1. Inventory review pages, external source links, citation records, numeric claims, figures, and generated data; identify which draft version each item describes.
  2. Check claims against primary sources where available, otherwise against the cited publisher page or a preserved local copy. Record source locators, method, date, access limits, and what the source does not establish.
  3. Recalculate item counts and graph totals from source registers; run exact-term searches against the current draft PDFs and manually check the relevant pages.
  4. Correct or remove unsupported statements and figures. Keep older findings only when clearly labeled as version-specific or method-limited.
  5. Publish the audit registers, this report, and the remaining work list; then check internal links, structured data, hashes, and the published site build.

Scope and corrections

  • Older 1,512-item crosswalks, 1,468/44 coverage results, predecessor dispositions, and graph relationships describe the first-round PDFs only. The current graph is labeled accordingly and does not represent the October 26 text.
  • Earlier coded-text totals and percentages were withdrawn after the audit found a two-token boundary artifact in one first-round benchmark register row and could not reproduce the parent-description word count. They remain only in the audit register as withdrawn values. Whole-PDF extraction totals are separately labeled as first-round packet measures and are not presented as comparable standards requirements or workload.
  • Earlier claims that the current draft omitted all biological-evolution wording, common ancestry, Earth’s age, or the universe’s age were corrected. Current public pages distinguish coded benchmark statements from clarifications and tasks, and explain that FDOE’s foreword says those supporting materials carry the full weight of the draft’s expected outcomes. Their eventual legal status depends on the adopted rule and incorporated files.
  • “Climate change” remains outside coded parent and leaf wording in the October 26 PDFs, but the broader current-version comparison of climate mechanisms, attribution, and forecasting tasks is not complete. No current whole-draft conclusion is drawn from the first-round task crosswalk.
  • Florida’s current Rule 6A-1.09401, effective August 3, 2026, lists the 2016 science standards among its incorporated references. The October 14, 2026 notice begins development to consider newly revised standards identified during review; it is not adoption of the 2026 draft. See the current final rule record and October 14 development notice.

Generative-AI role and human review

OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex assisted with source discovery, extraction and organization, crosswalk checks, count reproduction, drafting, and editing. AI research agents performed bounded reviews of public claims, analytical data, and bibliography/inline citations. Their work supplied candidate findings and audit tables; it is not independent human replication. The project author directed the work and remains responsible for the published analysis; this audit has not received independent human replication or a separate human sign-off on each correction. AI output was treated as a lead, not as evidence. The author and acknowledgements disclosure describes the broader workflow and limits.

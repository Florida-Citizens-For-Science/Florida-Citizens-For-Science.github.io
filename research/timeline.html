Florida science standards review timeline

The project author confirms that work on the review began October 22, 2026. This chronology records checkable public-artifact milestones from that start, along with source checks and repository history; it does not capture every off-repository research activity.

  1. October 22, 2026

    Review work began; research snapshot and public-record check

    The project author confirms that work began on October 22. The main comparison and corpus/process reports identify that date as the first research cutoff and record a check of the FDOE public-review portal and SHA-256 comparison showing the five preserved draft PDFs matched the linked portal copies that day.

    Corpus, process, and metadata report
  2. October 23, 2026

    Review hub and core analyses published

    Repository history records the standards-review hub and successive additions on 2008 and external-source comparisons, omission review, evolution and sectarian framing, provenance, human and generative-AI disclosures, public summaries, learning order, and teacher/student burden methods.

    The Florida Citizens for Science organization site and standards-review section were published on GitHub Pages, with forwarding links from the earlier personal-site location.

    Knowledge graph expanded and update rules consolidated

    The graph added all 498 Florida 2008 benchmark IDs, all 208 NGSS Performance Expectations, all 412 Indiana K–8 statements, ten complete grade-level blocks from the 2007 Florida Citizens for Science proposal, and fifteen ancillary document records. It now labels 3,354 machine-ranked similarity leads separately from reviewed evidence, records the original FCAT workbooks, and updates corpus coverage notes. A shared update-rules guide now covers timeline, corpus, graph, report, disclosure, and publication updates.

  8. October 24, 2026

    Omission analysis adds instructional-integrity implications

    The full report, plain-language brief, rhetoric audit, and omission crosswalk now explain how concentrated evolution and climate omissions may fragment explanatory learning and shift synthesis work to local teachers and materials. The analysis distinguishes these pedagogical risks from proof of classroom outcomes, acknowledges retained climate and evolution content, and cites the National Academies K–12 framework and the 2024 U.S. Climate Literacy Guide. Changes are recorded in commit 55c2479.

    Placement crosswalk updated with omissions and teaching consequences

    The evolution/climate placement page now identifies omitted evolution expectations and climate-attribution/forecasting tasks in its takeaways, explains possible consequences for coherent and accurate teaching, and corrects stale register totals to the current 1,512-item inventory. Changes are recorded in commit 7a56ebb.

    Read the updated placement crosswalk
  10. October 24, 2026

    Political-history evidence and project bibliography published

    The review foregrounds the history while naming the substantive omissions in the overview, comparison, subject analyses, plain-language brief, commentary, and shareable posts: no numbered evolution benchmark or common-ancestry synthesis; no equivalent human-evolution expectations; and no numbered climate-change benchmark or explicit climate-attribution/forecasting tasks. A project-wide bibliography includes publisher and DOI links, expandable source notes and BibTeX records, and a consolidated download. Local source copies and hashes are preserved in the research corpus. These records make the omissions politically salient but do not establish the 2026 workgroup’s motive. Published in commit 91a7b7e; the GitHub Pages build succeeded.

    Review landing page reorganized as a visual walkthrough

    The review landing page was reorganized as a six-chapter visual walkthrough covering the draft, evolution and climate omissions, benchmark coverage, an explanatory topic map, Florida’s adoption history, and evidence limits. A chapter navigator marks the current section; detailed pages open in new tabs so readers can return to their place. Published in commit b6cf66b; the GitHub Pages build succeeded.

    Open the visual walkthrough
  14. October 27, 2026

    Portal mirror, form reconciliation, and omission map expanded

    A dated local monitor now preserves the FDOE review page, link inventory, and five active PDFs; every PDF SHA-256 matches the existing corpus. Visible text was captured from all 26 online forms across dated runs and targeted retries, with 25 complete captures and one K–5 Part 3 capture partial at the 60-page limit. The review found 11 K–12 STARS standards absent from the grade-band PDFs and tracks them separately from the 1,512 leaf benchmarks. The overview and crosswalk now also identify Earth’s age and universe age/origin as notable omissions while distinguishing omitted conclusions from retained dating methods and cosmological evidence. A local timer is active for daily checks through October 31, then weekly.

    Read the portal monitoring roadmap · Read the omission crosswalk
  15. October 27, 2026

    Coded-text comparison corrected; adoption boundary clarified

    The comparison now distinguishes leaf benchmarks from coded parent-standard descriptions and separates both from review-packet guidance. Leaf wording is about 16,403 words in 2026 versus 15,675 in 2008 (+4.6%); parent-plus-leaf coded text is about 23,712 versus 18,875 (+25.6%). Counts are descriptive text volume, not target count or workload. The review adds Florida Statutes §1003.41, current Rule 6A-1.09401, and the October 14 rule-development notice to its source listing. It treats embedded clarifications conditionally because final legal status depends on the exact science rule and incorporated version, and adds a version-monitoring test for whether support text changes at adoption. No intent is inferred from a possible change.

    Read the source-layer analysis
  16. October 27, 2026

    Draft workload claim examined against foreword and statutes

    The review added Dr. Victor Sampson’s instructional-time analysis to the bibliography and checked its central claims against the proposed foreword and Florida graduation-credit statutes. The foreword confirms that clarifications and examples are part of the draft’s intended outcomes, while also calling for integrated instruction. The article’s fixed-period-per-clarification and all-benchmarks-in-three-credits calculations are retained as scenarios, not findings of a measured statewide workload shortfall.

    Read the source note
  17. October 27, 2026

    Landing page adds standards and source-document size graphics

    The walkthrough now explains that the proposed foreword treats benchmarks, clarifications, and examples as expected outcomes carrying the full weight of the draft. It separates a like-for-like coded-text comparison (23,712 words for 2026 versus 18,875 for 2008) from the whole 2026 packet and a comparative chart of selected source documents. The chart labels document scope, tokenization, and limitations; it does not present file size as target count or teaching time.

    Read the size and expectations chapter
  18. October 29, 2026

    Franklin Standards obtained, indexed, and compared as a candidate source

    The NAS-hosted 133-page Franklin Standards PDF and extracted text were preserved privately with hashes, fully indexed in the offline corpus, and added page by page to the private graph. Five reviewed topic comparisons now cover evolution, climate change, Earth age, and universe age; an eight-word sequence screen found no distinctive extended match. Public materials cite and summarize the copyrighted source without redistributing its text. Related site revisions were published in commit 94af428.

    Read the source comparison
  19. October 29, 2026

    Public notice and reactions summarized; sources added to project bibliography

    The review added a source-by-source summary of FDOE’s September–October notice and October 27 public hearing, plus selected reactions reported by WUSF, WLRN, Central Florida Public Media, and NCSE. Eight new verified references were added to the project bibliography and consolidated BibTeX. The current FDOE portal gives a month-range but no day-specific written-comment deadline; October 29 reporting records the Department’s nearly 1,800-comment figure and mixed views at the final session. The summary labels statements by source and does not treat them as representative public opinion. Published in commit 8ee8e9f.
